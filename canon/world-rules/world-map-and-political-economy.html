World Map and Political Economy

One-Line Summary

After the White Sky, the world is no longer cleanly divided into nations and megacorps. It is divided into maintained territories, sacrificed territories, and contested territories — and every faction claims a different right to decide which is which.

Canon Status

Soft-canon seed. This page proposes the large-scale shape of the Neon Champions world. It should guide future faction, location, campaign-map, and conflict work, but specific borders, dates, and named powers remain open.

Core Principle: The Map Became Layered

The old political map still exists. Flags, armies, passports, languages, elections, treaties, and national myths still matter.

But everyday sovereignty is layered over it:

  • a national border;
  • a climate-service territory;
  • an insurer risk zone;
  • a corporate concession;
  • a platform identity jurisdiction;
  • a private-security service area;
  • a compute/energy priority district;
  • a water compact;
  • a migration permit zone;
  • a Digital-Net shard or Free-Net corridor.

A person can cross ten kinds of border without leaving one city.

This is what makes the world feel unlike ours without becoming random. The future did not abolish states. It made citizenship compete with service access.

The Three Territory Classes

Maintained Territories

Maintained territories are places where the systems still work because someone pays, profits, governs, insures, or fights to keep them working.

They may be rich states, corporate regions, port corridors, arcologies, datacenter basins, protected deltas, elite city cores, biosecure health zones, or strategic farms.

People there do not necessarily live freely. They live inside maintenance contracts.

Common traits:

  • reliable cooling, filtration, drainage, power, and feed provenance;
  • high surveillance and behavioral scoring;
  • body-subscription integration with work, insurance, and residency;
  • private security alongside public law;
  • clean public spaces with hidden coercion;
  • bright climate dashboards and carefully managed sky narratives.

Moral ambiguity: maintained territories save lives. They also turn survival into a subscription.

Sacrificed Territories

Sacrificed territories are not always abandoned. Many are densely inhabited. They are places the dominant systems have decided not to fully protect.

They include salinity belts, heat basins, fire corridors, uninsurable suburbs, failed polders, informal settlements outside cooling grids, polluted extraction districts, and migration holding zones.

Common traits:

  • emergency alerts instead of infrastructure;
  • mobile clinics instead of permanent health systems;
  • cheap or experimental body modifications;
  • polluted feeds, thin bandwidth, and fake relief dashboards;
  • informal water, power, and cooling markets;
  • higher mutation, disease, and violence risk;
  • Free-Net archives and rumors replacing official truth.

Moral ambiguity: sacrificed territories are not passive victim zones. They produce resistance, culture, black markets, Champions, and alternate institutions.

Contested Territories

Contested territories are places whose future classification is still being fought over.

They include deltas with half-built seawalls, Arctic routes, dam corridors, cable chokepoints, mineral basins, city receiverships, launch sites, refugee work-towns, and White Sky maintenance corridors.

Common traits:

  • overlapping claims;
  • infrastructure sabotage;
  • propaganda battles over weather, crime, casualty, and model data;
  • private-security patrols beside militias and state forces;
  • Champions used as negotiators, warlords, celebrities, or living flags;
  • unstable legal status.

Moral ambiguity: contested territories are where the game-world breathes. They contain opportunity, danger, and factional arguments.

What Changed Natural Borders

Water Moved

Coasts did not vanish uniformly. They became expensive to maintain.

The important new borders are seawalls, pump districts, salinity buffers, elevated rail spines, insurer red lines, port-security zones, and evacuation tiers. Some districts are dry but financially dead. Some are wet but economically alive because they contain ports, cables, or protected cores.

Delta regions are especially unstable. The same river mouth can contain a corporate port, a national rice district, a drowned suburb, a floating black market, a military desalination intake, and a sacred village refusing relocation.

Heat Became Law

In hot-humid regions, borders are seasonal and temporal. A road may be legal at night and deadly by noon. Work may be permitted only under cooling credits. Migration may depend on body heat-tolerance certification.

Heat law creates new status markers:

  • cooling citizenship;
  • night-worker permits;
  • shade-corridor passes;
  • hydration firmware;
  • modified lungs, kidneys, skin, endocrine systems;
  • heat-debt owed to employers or clinics.

Rivers Became Military Systems

Glacier retreat, snowpack change, dams, floods, drought years, and hydropower make rivers into weaponized infrastructure.

The water border is not just the river channel. It is the dam gate, reservoir algorithm, flood forecast, sediment flow, desalination contract, irrigation quota, and insurance model.

The Arctic Became a Risk Frontier

The Arctic is not an open paradise. It is a dangerous, partially accessible, sensor-saturated frontier where shipping, cables, minerals, fisheries, indigenous sovereignty, military patrols, and climate-control infrastructure collide.

Some powers want more ice preserved. Some want access. Some want both, depending on the quarter.

The Sky Became Territory

The white-sky makes atmosphere into a governed layer.

White Sky maintenance corridors, cloud-brightening seas, aerosol launch sites, orbital sensors, weather-model basins, and public-risk dashboards become strategic geography. People fight over the sky because it has owners, operators, liabilities, and casualties.

Corporate Power Without Cartoon Megacorps

Corporate regions exist, but the strongest version is not “a corporation bought a country and became king.”

The stronger pattern is:

  1. A disaster makes normal public finance impossible.
  2. A rescue package funds cooling, pumps, clinics, ports, grids, or housing.
  3. The funder receives a special jurisdiction, concession, arbitration protection, data rights, and emergency operating authority.
  4. Private security protects the infrastructure.
  5. Residents become dependent on the service stack.
  6. Reversal remains legal but would break the budget, the grid, the seawall, the clinic system, or the food supply.

Officially, sovereignty remains national. Practically, daily life is corporate-administered.

This allows corporations to be powerful, frightening, and sometimes genuinely useful without reducing the setting to moustache-twirling villains.

Corporate Concessions and Service Territories

The phrase “corporate archipelago” can be useful analytically, but the in-world names should be more grounded: concessions, service territories, receiver districts, port authorities, bonded cloud zones, work-residency zones, and stabilization compacts.

The corporate map is discontinuous. It looks like beads, corridors, ports, campuses, basins, towers, mines, launch territories, cloud districts, health zones, and migration work-towns scattered across national maps.

Resilience Arcology Districts

Insurer-construction-utility consortia administer flood/fire/heat-safe districts. Residents get protection and verified services. They lose privacy, land autonomy, and unmanaged politics.

Bonded Cloud / Compute Concessions

Cloud firms, power producers, and sovereign funds control datacenter regions with energy priority, water rights, VI allocation, model-weight custody, and data-sovereignty carve-outs. “Compute freeport” is the generic policy category; actual places should have grounded names such as Model Freeport, Bonded Cloud Zone, Sovereign Cloud Concession, Data Entrepôt, or Compute Concession. Residents receive jobs and stability but live inside compute rationing and behavior scores.

Biosecure Health Zones

Biotech and hospital operators run pathogen-controlled districts, organ farms, gene clinics, fertility services, and body-subscription infrastructure. Safety and bodily control are inseparable.

Critical Minerals Protectorates

Mining firms, security contractors, export-credit agencies, and host ministries administer mineral basins. Roads, clinics, and wages come with extraction, tailings, surveillance, and insurgency.

Port, Cable, and Logistics Corridors

Terminal operators, customs platforms, telecom consortia, and private police control trade arteries. These places decide what arrives, what leaves, whose feed is clean, and whose money clears.

Climate Migration Work-Towns

Employers provide visas, dorms, clinics, food, remittance rails, and cooling. Losing work can mean losing residency, medicine, and family access.

Global Blocs and Map Logics

These are not final faction names. They are world-map pressures that can later produce factions, alliances, campaign regions, and conflicts.

North Atlantic Climate Compact

A law-insurance-finance-compute civilization built around climate liability, certified feeds, Arctic access, offshore wind, nuclear/hydro power, and regulation.

Heroic face: preserves due process, archives, climate courts, and public accountability.

Horrific face: exports actuarial exclusion, debt receivership, sensor mandates, and polite abandonment.

White Sky stance: controlled maintenance, strict liability, fear of termination shock, anxiety over North Atlantic circulation changes.

Visual identity: pale sky over glass floodgates, polished court towers, insurer seals, public dashboards, memorials to saved districts and abandoned ones.

Pacific Compute-Manufacturing Mesh

A chain of fabs, cable hubs, ports, robotics centers, battery plants, and trusted hardware zones.

Heroic face: technical competence, manufacturing depth, resilient logistics, disciplined reconstruction.

Horrific face: labor discipline, supply-chain caste, water sacrifice, and zero-tolerance security.

White Sky stance: divided; it protects coasts but threatens monsoons, solar yield, and fab-water stability.

Visual identity: cleanrooms, sea walls, cable bunkers, drone ports, neon against white daytime haze.

Equatorial Grievance Belt

A broad set of harmed, exploited, resource-rich, youthful, and politically divergent regions across wet tropics, mineral belts, rainforests, islands, and heat-exposed cities.

Heroic face: climate justice, resource sovereignty, demographic vitality, anti-extraction politics, new cultures of adaptation.

Horrific face: concession elites, armed resource politics, bio-extraction, hostage migration corridors, and factional fragmentation.

White Sky stance: often hostile; accuses sky operators of solar theft, monsoon theft, and climate occupation.

Visual identity: humid glare, storm walls, copper/cobalt roads, solar protests, body-clinic queues, blue-paint slogans.

Desert Desalination and Solar States

High-control states and corporate cities that manufacture habitability through desalination, cooling, sovereign wealth, helium/LNG, solar, ports, and migrant labor.

Heroic face: engineering mastery, managed survival, high-tech refuge, water production.

Horrific face: labor dependence, heat apartheid, citizenship tiers, sealed luxury under lethal skies.

White Sky stance: resents dimming and may demand compensation or local tuning authority.

Visual identity: mirrored shade canopies, desalination cathedrals, night markets, sealed transit, white-sky glare on solar fields.

Arctic-Subarctic Frontier

A dangerous northern frontier of shipping, cables, minerals, cold datacenters, fisheries, launch/sensor stations, military patrols, and indigenous sovereignty.

Heroic face: new autonomy movements, cold refuge, hard survival, sensor science, route rescue.

Horrific face: extraction rush, militarized insurance, displaced communities, private navies, black-ice disasters.

White Sky stance: ambivalent; cooling protects some systems while limiting access and extraction.

Visual identity: white sky merging with snow haze, black water, drone buoys, icebreaker convoys, undersea cable shrines.

Continental Granaries and Nutrient States

Food, fertilizer, seed IP, water timing, autonomous agriculture, fermentation protein, and sewage nutrient recovery become strategic power.

Heroic face: feeds people, preserves soil, protects local agriculture.

Horrific face: calorie diplomacy, seed DRM, nutrient surveillance, export coercion.

White Sky stance: practical; supports whichever sky regime stabilizes yields.

Visual identity: pale crop fields, autonomous harvesters, nutrient police, grain-port fortresses, mycoprotein vats.

Free-Net and Exclusion Commons

A non-state layer of abandoned, uninsurable, insurgent, mesh-networked, floating, underground, and disaster-scarred territories.

Heroic face: refuge, archive, mutual aid, black-market medicine, anti-corporate truth work.

Horrific face: scams, cults, malware, gang rule, bad body hacks, false memories, poisoned feeds.

White Sky stance: remembers what official systems erase, but not cleanly.

Visual identity: illegal antennas, scavenged solar skins, broken flood walls, community cooling shrines, graffiti maps, dead-mall vats, Free-Net reliquaries.

Wars That Fit This World

Corridor Wars

Conflicts over ports, rail spines, cable landings, repair fleets, dam roads, desalination pipelines, launch corridors, and HVDC routes.

They are limited enough for corporate/state actors to deny escalation, but strategically decisive.

Maintenance Wars

Conflicts over White Sky infrastructure, cooling grids, pumps, filters, sensor basins, and climate-model custody.

The objective is rarely conquest. It is to control whose life-support remains credible.

Actuarial Wars

Conflicts fought through insurance withdrawal, credit downgrades, disaster-model leaks, risk-score sabotage, and parametric payout manipulation.

A city can be destroyed financially before a missile lands.

Body Rights Wars

Conflicts over whether leased bodies, prosthetics, gene packages, reproductive services, immune systems, and organ support can be repossessed, downgraded, inherited, hacked, or nationalized.

Champions can emerge as symbols of bodily sovereignty.

Provenance Wars

Conflicts over which feed records are believed: casualty counts, weather data, atrocity footage, land titles, election results, migration claims, and Echo continuity.

Reality itself becomes a supply chain.

Nutrient and Calorie Wars

Conflicts over fertilizer, grain routes, seed DRM, synthetic-protein vats, fishing grounds, and urban nutrient recovery.

These wars feel less glamorous than chip wars but may decide mass legitimacy.

Role of Champions in This Map

Champions should be more than super-units. They are people whose bodies, reputations, Echoes, or battlefield actions can puncture layered sovereignty.

A Champion can be:

  • a walking legal crisis whose body violates ownership categories;
  • a celebrity whose presence stabilizes a sacrificed territory;
  • a factional saint of Blue Restoration, Sky Loyalty, body sovereignty, Free-Net truth, or corporate salvation;
  • a deniable corporate asset leased across jurisdictions;
  • a local warlord who controls a cooling corridor;
  • a person whose Echo proves or destabilizes a disputed past;
  • a symbol strong enough to override actuarial classifications and platform bans.

The world needs Champions because ordinary institutions have become too layered, deniable, and technical for people to believe in. Champions are visible proof that history still has faces.

Visual / Sensory Identity

  • A permanent pale sky, bright enough to feel exposed and false.
  • Neon and holograms in daytime, not just night, because the world needs color against white glare.
  • Blue as luxury, nostalgia, contraband, therapy, or rebellion.
  • Seawalls, pump towers, heat shelters, cooling stacks, drone buoys, cable bunkers, and body clinics as everyday landmarks.
  • Rich territories are over-maintained: polished filters, verified feeds, smooth transit, climate dashboards, quiet private security.
  • Poor territories are over-measured: risk warnings, debt screens, cheap sensors, unreliable apps, emergency sirens, broken shade nets.
  • Corporate borders marked by service quality: the air changes, the feed cleans up, the drones become quieter, the water tastes different.
  • Sacrificed zones are not empty wastelands. They are dense with improvisation, cults, repairs, archives, bad medicine, and desperate beauty.

In-World Beliefs

  • Sky Loyalists: the White Sky is ugly life support; opposing it is mass murder.
  • Blue Restorationists: the White Sky is corporate occupation of the atmosphere.
  • Albedo Sovereignists: no distant actor should tune another region’s weather.
  • Corporate Humanists: private life-support saved civilization where states failed.
  • Receivership Realists: democracy without pumps, power, and clinics is theatre.
  • Free-Net Archivists: official climate truth is curated liability management.
  • Body Sovereigns: survival modifications must become rights, not subscriptions.
  • Nutrient Nationalists: food, waste, seed, and soil are the real sovereignty.
  • Arctic Openers: cooling robbed the north of its future access.
  • Heat Justice movements: habitable hours are a human right.

Open Questions

  • Should the default campaign map show national borders first, or maintenance/sacrifice/contest zones first?
  • Which bloc is strongest in the starting year?
  • Which region benefited most from White Sky and therefore defends it sincerely?
  • Which region was most visibly harmed by White Sky and therefore supplies the strongest opposition?
  • Are corporate regions normalized everywhere, or concentrated in former/debt-distressed developmental economies plus disaster-hit rich cities?
  • Should one corporation be the first White Sky mover, or should its identity remain hidden behind a rescue consortium?
  • Do players mostly command factions inside one region, or move across the global corporate archipelago?
