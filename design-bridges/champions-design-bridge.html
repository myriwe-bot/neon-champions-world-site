Player-Facing Decision

The first design assumption is:

Champions are primarily HoMM3-style hero/commander units: strategic map leaders who command armies, grow over time, carry equipment/bodyware/relic-equivalents, project faction identity, and anchor the player’s emotional attachment to a force.

They are not just powerful battlefield units. They are the interface between army, faction, territory, reputation, body modification, and story.

Core Player Fantasy

A Champion is the person who makes an army feel like your army.

The player fantasy is not “I have a superhero who solos the map.” It is:

  • I recruit or win over a historically loaded figure.
  • I build an army around their doctrine, body, reputation, and constraints.
  • I risk them on the map because their presence changes morale, logistics, scouting, legitimacy, and combat.
  • I watch them become famous, compromised, injured, mythologized, or replaced.
  • I decide whether to treat them as a person, a weapon, a symbol, an investment, or a martyr.

Design Role

Primary Role: Strategic Hero / Commander

Champions should fill the HoMM3 hero niche:

  • move across the strategic map;
  • command unit stacks or squads;
  • explore territory;
  • capture sites;
  • carry upgrades and artifacts-equivalents;
  • gain experience;
  • specialize through skills;
  • represent faction doctrine;
  • trigger events, diplomacy, and story hooks.

Secondary Role: Faction Signal

Each faction’s Champions should reveal what that faction believes humanity should become.

Examples:

  • corporate stabilization faction: audited celebrity commanders, insured bodies, media-trained presence;
  • body-sovereignty faction: scarred self-owned mutation leaders, unstable but hard to repossess;
  • Free-Net faction: low-visibility archive ghosts, sensor spoofers, dead-zone guides;
  • Blue Restorationists: sabotage saints and sky martyrs;
  • human-exceptionalist leagues: charismatic anti-AI commanders sold as proof that people still matter;
  • machine-emergence sects: VI-haunted commanders whose autonomy is always suspect.

Tertiary Role: Public / Reputation Object

Because of chorus-exchange, Champions also have publics. A Champion’s fame, scandal, martyrdom, and monetization can become strategic pressure.

This should be secondary at first. Do not let fame systems replace the core hero/commander role.

What Champions Are Not, Mechanically

Champions should not initially be:

  • direct fantasy spellcasters;
  • generic RPG party members;
  • simple “super units” that replace armies;
  • interchangeable stat sticks;
  • purely narrative NPCs with no strategic map function;
  • every important character in the setting.

A Champion is a commander first. Their body, myth, and public matter because those things modify command.

Baseline Champion Model

Each Champion should have these design-facing fields:

FieldPurpose
OriginHow they became recognized: war, biotech, media, mutation, cult, corporation, city, Net event.
Faction RelationshipOwned, sponsored, allied, coerced, defected, illegal, folk-recognized, or self-claimed.
Command DoctrineWhat kind of army they make stronger.
Body / InterfaceBiological, prosthetic, neural, VI-assisted, Echo-linked, unmodified but symbolically powerful.
PublicWho follows, fears, funds, worships, or hates them.
CostWhat must be paid to keep them effective.
Failure ModeHow they break, defect, burn out, get exposed, become obsolete, or become dangerous.
Strategic HookWhat map/combat/economy decision they create.

Candidate Stat Categories

Avoid final numbers now. Use categories for design exploration.

Command

How effectively the Champion coordinates units under stress.

Possible effects:

  • army morale;
  • initiative;
  • formation recovery;
  • retreat discipline;
  • mixed-unit coordination;
  • resistance to panic, feed corruption, or VI spoofing.

Reach

How far the Champion’s operational influence extends.

Possible effects:

  • map movement;
  • scouting radius;
  • relay use;
  • remote detachment control;
  • ability to operate through degraded networks.

Presence

How strongly the Champion affects human behavior.

Possible effects:

  • recruitment;
  • surrender chance;
  • intimidation;
  • revolt suppression or ignition;
  • public morale;
  • enemy obsession.

Interface

How well the Champion works with systems.

Possible effects:

  • drones;
  • battlefield mesh;
  • Axiom-certified records;
  • Free-Net fragments;
  • cyberwar resistance;
  • Echo or VI adjacency.

Body

How far the Champion’s physical substrate has been pushed.

Possible effects:

  • survival;
  • injury recovery;
  • hostile-climate operation;
  • dueling;
  • mutation risk;
  • upkeep burden.

Legitimacy

How accepted the Champion is as a real commander, symbol, or legal person.

Possible effects:

  • access to city contracts;
  • militia loyalty;
  • faction diplomacy;
  • Chorus ranking;
  • Axiom recognition;
  • black-market bounties.

Progression Model

A Champion should progress along several axes, not just gain combat stats.

Experience

Traditional campaign/combat experience. Unlocks doctrine, command, and survival improvements.

Bodyware / Grafts / Protocols

Artifact-equivalent slot system.

Possible slot types:

  • neural command lattice;
  • climate organ lease;
  • prosthetic combat frame;
  • battlefield sensor crown;
  • immune stabilizer;
  • memory/Echo co-processor;
  • drone-swarm interface;
  • ceremonial relic with morale value;
  • Axiom-certified identity seal;
  • Chorus public contract.

These replace fantasy artifacts, spellbooks, and enchanted gear.

Reputation

Public recognition and fear.

Can improve recruitment, morale, diplomacy, and intimidation, but also increase targeting, scandal risk, bounty pressure, and loss impact.

Doctrine

Faction-specific command tree.

Examples:

  • swarm warfare;
  • insurgent mobility;
  • body horror shock troops;
  • civil-defense resilience;
  • feed manipulation;
  • logistics denial;
  • polar survival;
  • climate-zone adaptation;
  • anti-Champion discipline.

Dependency

The hidden cost track.

A Champion may depend on:

  • a clinic contract;
  • body-subscription maintenance;
  • compute allocation;
  • Chorus public liquidity;
  • faction legitimacy;
  • Axiom recognition;
  • drugs/stabilizers;
  • private handlers;
  • Echo reconstruction;
  • battlefield telemetry.

Progress should make Champions stronger but also more entangled.

Equipment / Artifact Equivalents

Fantasy artifacts become disputed, maintainable cyberpunk objects.

Candidate categories:

  1. Bodyware — organs, prosthetics, glands, combat frames, reflex grafts.
  2. Command Interfaces — tactical crowns, war-OS keys, drone relays, encrypted field desks.
  3. Identity Seals — Axiom records, legal immunities, citizenship exceptions, treaty clearances.
  4. Public Contracts — Chorus ranking bands, Patron Channel access, sponsorship insulation, scandal insurance.
  5. Relics — dead Champion gear, battlefield flags, old war helmets, Echo-bearing objects.
  6. Climate Adaptations — lungs, skin, cooling packs, radiation/chemical filters, zone-specific body licenses.
  7. Net Keys — Free-Net maps, municipal sensor backdoors, dead archive access, hostile model containment tokens.

Each item should have:

  • benefit;
  • maintenance cost;
  • legal risk;
  • faction reaction;
  • narrative hook.

Army Relationship

Champions should not replace armies. They should reshape armies.

A Champion can:

  • raise unit capacity;
  • unlock faction unit variants;
  • improve certain unit families;
  • make weak units strategically useful;
  • enable mixed-force coordination;
  • reduce terrain/climate penalties;
  • provide combat-start doctrine bonuses;
  • affect post-battle casualties, capture, retreat, or propaganda.

A Champion should also create vulnerabilities:

  • assassination target;
  • morale collapse if killed or exposed;
  • faction schism if they defect;
  • upkeep burden;
  • dependency on specific infrastructure;
  • scandal or de-legitimization by Chorus/Axiom;
  • body maintenance crises.

Death, Injury, and Echoes

Champion loss should matter more than ordinary unit loss.

Possible states:

  • Wounded — temporarily weakened, needs clinic/repair/stabilizer.
  • Burned Out — alive but loses access to some abilities until dependency is resolved.
  • Captured — ransom, propaganda, forced conversion, body extraction, Echo risk.
  • Dead — removed physically, but their public may persist.
  • Echoed — partial continuity through echo-identity-and-death, legally contested and mechanically changed.
  • Martyred — death strengthens public/faction but removes command function.
  • Exposed as Manufactured — not dead, but legitimacy collapses.

Do not make resurrection easy. Echo continuity should be powerful, partial, expensive, and politically dangerous.

Recognition / Certification

For gameplay, Champion status should have tiers of recognition.

Field-Recognized

Troops follow them. They may have no legal status.

Faction-Recognized

A faction treats them as a Champion and unlocks faction-specific mechanics.

Axiom-Recognized

Their identity, history, and command authority are legally admissible.

Chorus-Recognized

They have public reach, ranking, monetization, and scandal risk.

UNP / Treaty-Recognized

Rare top-tier Champions whose actions create diplomatic consequences.

These tiers let the game distinguish between folk heroes, corporate products, legal commanders, public idols, and treaty anomalies.

False, Failed, and Illegal Champions

These categories should be design tools, not edge lore.

False Champion

A manufactured or fraudulent Champion whose public exceeds their real command/body capacity.

Gameplay use:

  • morale and recruitment bonuses;
  • collapse if exposed;
  • strong Chorus interaction;
  • weak under direct battlefield pressure.

Failed Champion

A real candidate who survived alteration but broke physically, mentally, legally, or socially.

Gameplay use:

  • unstable powers;
  • high upkeep;
  • black-clinic or outlaw factions;
  • tragic recruitment events.

Illegal Champion

A commander whose body, VI interface, Echo status, or command method violates law or compact rules.

Gameplay use:

  • strong abilities;
  • Axiom/Helvetia/UNP pressure;
  • bounty and sanction risk.

Refused Champion

A person treated as a Champion despite rejecting the role.

Gameplay use:

  • strong story hooks;
  • loyalty and morale tension;
  • lower public-management control.

Possible Mechanics

Strategic Map

  • Champion movement points.
  • Terrain/climate affinity.
  • Control-zone or aura effects.
  • Site capture and negotiation.
  • Scouting and feed-integrity checks.
  • Champion-specific event chains.
  • Rival Champion tracking.

Army Management

  • Unit capacity.
  • Unit-family bonuses.
  • Doctrine unlocks.
  • Morale and cohesion.
  • Maintenance costs.
  • Injury recovery.
  • Compatibility problems with certain units.

Combat

  • Pre-battle doctrine choice.
  • Limited active command abilities, grounded as orders, bodyware, drones, feed hacks, or morale shocks.
  • Duel or confrontation events, if appropriate.
  • Emergency retreat / sacrifice choices.
  • Public consequences after major victories or defeats.

Campaign / Narrative

  • Defection events.
  • Scandal attacks.
  • Body repossession threats.
  • Echo inheritance disputes.
  • Fan cult incidents.
  • Axiom certification challenges.
  • Chorus ranking manipulation.
  • Illegal upgrade opportunities.

Anti-Magic Translation

HoMM3 magic equivalents should become command-tech, body-tech, network operations, logistics, and public effects.

Examples:

Fantasy FunctionNeon Champions Translation
Hastestimulant protocol, drone-guided routing, predictive command burst.
Slowfeed corruption, terrain denial, logistics choke, fear broadcast.
Blessmorale ritual, legal immunity, verified heroic footage, command sync.
Cursescandal packet, Axiom dispute, misinformation fog, bodyware instability.
Resurrectionbattlefield triage, organ packs, drone extraction, contested Echo restoration.
Town PortalJanus-Kestrel emergency clearance, bonded corridor priority, illicit relay jump.
VisionFree-Net archive map, satellite/civic sensor access, Axiom-certified reconnaissance.

This keeps the design inheritance from HoMM3 without adding direct magic.

Initial Champion Archetypes

These are design placeholders, not final characters.

The War-OS Commander

A Vezha-derived commander whose value is coordination under jamming, blackout, and drone saturation.

  • Strong with drones, militias, civil-defense units.
  • Weakness: dependence on battlefield telemetry and legal war-OS licenses.
  • Risk: accused of being VI-commanded rather than human-led.

The Climate Saint

A White Sky / Blue Week figure whose body and public are tied to climate survival or restoration.

  • Strong in climate-stressed territories.
  • Inspires loyalists or restorationists depending on interpretation.
  • Weakness: body upkeep and propaganda vulnerability.

The Body Sovereign

A self-owned or black-clinic Champion who rejects subscription flesh.

  • Strong with irregulars, mutation units, anti-corporate factions.
  • Weakness: instability, limited legal support, difficult healing.

The Chorus Idol-Commander

A media-native Champion whose public funds and follows their campaigns.

  • Strong recruitment and morale.
  • Can convert victories into resources.
  • Weakness: scandal, overexposure, public backlash.

The Free-Net Warden

A low-visibility Champion who navigates archives, dead zones, polluted feeds, and sensor ghosts.

  • Strong scouting, deception resistance, hidden routes.
  • Weakness: poor public legitimacy and limited formal command.

The Echo-Bound Veteran

A commander with partial Echo continuity or dead-comrade advisory layers.

  • Strong memory, resilience, morale around veteran units.
  • Weakness: legal/personhood disputes and possible AGI Compact scrutiny.

Key Risks

  1. Champion bloat — if every named character is a Champion, the category loses force.
  2. Superhero drift — direct powers can break the no-magic / cyberpunk grounding.
  3. Commander irrelevance — if Champions only fight personally, the HoMM3 inheritance is lost.
  4. Fame overcomplexity — Chorus systems should enrich, not swamp, the core hero loop.
  5. Easy resurrection — Echoes should complicate loss, not erase it.
  6. Faction sameness — each faction’s Champions need different doctrine, upkeep, and moral cost.

Decisions Needed

  1. Should every player-controlled hero be a Champion, or should some commanders be non-Champion officers?
  2. Should Champion combat include personal active abilities, or mostly army-wide command effects?
  3. How many Champions should a faction plausibly field at one time?
  4. Should Champion equipment/bodyware be swappable like artifacts, or partly permanent/scarring?
  5. Can a Champion be permanently killed, or always converted into some aftereffect?
  6. Should Chorus fame be a visible game resource in the first prototype, or a later campaign layer?

Recommended Next Design Step

Create a small prototype-facing Champion schema with 3 sample Champions:

  1. one corporate/stabilization commander;
  2. one rebel/body-sovereignty commander;
  3. one Free-Net or Echo-adjacent commander.

Each should include:

  • faction fit;
  • map role;
  • army bonuses;
  • bodyware/artifact slots;
  • upkeep/dependency;
  • failure state;
  • one narrative event hook.
