Player-Facing Design Premise

The strategic campaign map is not “countries versus corporations.” It is a layered survival map where real states still exist, but daily life is controlled by maintenance contracts, corporate concessions, risk zones, feed provenance, migration permissions, water compacts, compute districts, and White Sky infrastructure.

The core player question:

Who gets maintained, who gets sacrificed, and who gets to decide before the crisis clocks run out?

The player should not simply conquer territory. They should fight over infrastructure, proof, legitimacy, corridors, Champions, and the right to define what a region is.

Design Role

Primary Role: Strategic Campaign Map Grammar

This bridge defines how a playable region is built:

  • real geography anchor;
  • formal sovereign actor;
  • actual daily-life controller;
  • maintenance status;
  • White Sky state;
  • corporate concession type;
  • corridor dependencies;
  • strategic sites;
  • active resources;
  • crisis clocks;
  • proof/evidence stakes;
  • Champion hooks;
  • victory options.

Secondary Role: Crisis Escalation Engine

Campaigns should be driven by interlocking clocks, not fixed apocalypse timers.

The player rarely “solves” the world. They slow, redirect, expose, exploit, or accelerate crises.

Tertiary Role: Faction and Champion Pressure System

Layered institutions are slow, deniable, technical, and compromised. Champions matter because they make the map human-readable: commander, witness, saint, scandal, warlord, mediator, saboteur, martyr, living legal crisis.

Core Map Layers

The campaign map should support layer toggles as gameplay, not just UI.

The guiding principle:

Which map is true depends on the decision the player is making.

1. Formal Sovereignty Layer

Real states, borders, capitals, militaries, passports, elections, treaties, and national myths.

Purpose:

  • keeps the world recognizable;
  • prevents abstract overlay names from replacing geography;
  • gives diplomacy, law, armies, and legitimacy a home.

Gameplay use:

  • access rights;
  • state armies;
  • treaties;
  • legal consequences;
  • public legitimacy;
  • nationalization options.

2. Maintenance Status Layer

The primary gameplay terrain class.

StatusMeaningTypical Effects
MaintainedSystems work because someone pays, profits, governs, insures, or fights to keep them working.Lower attrition, higher income, stronger security, cleaner feeds, higher surveillance/dependency.
SacrificedDominant systems have chosen not to fully protect the area, even if people remain.Higher hazard, lower formal income, irregular recruitment, Free-Net access, black markets, local Champions.
ContestedThe future maintenance status is being fought over.Volatile events, high rewards, overlapping claims, prime scenario space.

Design rule:

Maintained is not “good.” Sacrificed is not “empty.” Contested is where the game breathes.

3. White Sky / Climate-Service Layer

Use the states established in white-sky-and-meridian-stations.

StateMeaningCampaign Use
StabilizedCooling and services function.Lower hazard clocks; higher QXZ/legal presence.
Over-TunedIntervention is excessive or badly timed.Rainfall/solar/unrest penalties despite lower heat.
Under-MaintainedMaintenance is weak or delayed.Heat, disease, crop, and migration pressure rise.
DisputedModels disagree; attribution is contested.Proof missions, tribunals, protests, sabotage.
Broken / Blue BreakPale ceiling thins or fails temporarily.Morale shock, panic, religious/faction events, climate spike.
Rogue-TunedUnauthorized actor modifies sky conditions.Unpredictable bonuses/penalties, diplomatic crisis.

4. Corporate Concession Layer

Corporate power appears as discontinuous service territory, not one giant empire.

Examples:

  • receiver cities;
  • bonded cloud zones;
  • port continuity districts;
  • mineral concessions;
  • biosecure health zones;
  • climate migration work-towns;
  • White Sky maintenance concessions;
  • food/nutrient routing districts;
  • model freeports;
  • identity/provenance service areas.

Gameplay use:

  • special local rules;
  • private security;
  • contract access;
  • resource outputs;
  • service dependency;
  • nationalization or renegotiation events.

5. Corridor Layer

Corridors are roads plus resources plus political claims.

Candidate corridor types:

  • energy corridors;
  • compute corridors;
  • water/desalination routes;
  • food/nutrient routes;
  • ports and cable landings;
  • migration routes;
  • rail and repair spines;
  • dam and river-control roads;
  • White Sky launch/maintenance corridors;
  • medical/bodyware supply lines.

Gameplay use:

  • movement;
  • supply;
  • upkeep;
  • evacuation;
  • station operation;
  • legal access;
  • strategic chokepoints.

6. Feed / Provenance Layer

The information layer from digital-net-and-polluted-feeds.

Shows:

  • Axiom-admissible evidence;
  • Chorus narrative heat;
  • Free-Net archives;
  • polluted feeds;
  • disputed records;
  • targeted-accounting pressure;
  • false routes or contacts.

Gameplay use:

  • proof objectives;
  • scandal defense;
  • public legitimacy;
  • access to contracts or tribunals;
  • misinformation as map terrain.

7. Champion Incident Layer

Maps where history has faces.

Possible markers:

  • Champion origin sites;
  • martyrdom sites;
  • public duels;
  • failed interventions;
  • body-rights scandals;
  • Echo testimony sites;
  • places where a Champion broke accounting, stabilized a corridor, or caused a crisis.

Gameplay use:

  • recruitment;
  • morale;
  • event chains;
  • faction identity;
  • scenario objectives;
  • relic/bodyware/proof rewards.

Region Archetypes

Use these as campaign-region building blocks.

Receiver City

A city still formally governed by a mayor, council, or state, but cooling, flood defense, policing, emergency services, identity, insurance, and budget continuity are controlled by a receiver consortium.

Best use:

  • tutorial or early campaign region.

Mechanics:

  • split legitimacy;
  • service dependency;
  • contract gates;
  • strike/revolt events;
  • proof-based renegotiation;
  • private security alongside public law.

Port Continuity Corridor

A port, cable, logistics, customs, and flood-protection zone kept alive while surrounding neighborhoods are sacrificed.

Mechanics:

  • high logistics income;
  • migration choke point;
  • cable/provenance leverage;
  • dockworker unrest;
  • private navy/security;
  • floodline morality.

Rainfall Attribution District

A region where delayed monsoon, failed planting, flood pulses, seed loss, and model disputes create legal and insurgent conflict.

Mechanics:

  • evidence missions;
  • sensor sabotage;
  • compensation claims;
  • food instability;
  • militia recruitment;
  • White Sky blame contests.

Migration Work-Town Belt

Employer-run housing, labor, clinic, residency, food, remittance, and body-subscription zones.

Mechanics:

  • population flow;
  • permit control;
  • labor unrest;
  • bodyware dependency;
  • recruitment and repression choices;
  • family-separation stakes.

Mineral / Infrastructure Concession

Mine, refinery, rail, road, water, security, and export system tied to batteries, compute, White Sky, or grids.

Mechanics:

  • extraction income;
  • mutation/body harm;
  • sabotage risk;
  • private security;
  • Free-Net contract leaks;
  • local-state legitimacy conflict.

Bonded Cloud / Compute Concession

Strategic computation, model custody, climate-model hosting, VI allocation, chip attestation, and data-sovereignty carve-outs.

Mechanics:

  • compute resource;
  • energy/water competition;
  • sanctions risk;
  • cyber/provenance actions;
  • Helvetia or state oversight;
  • AI Compact suspicion.

Biosecure Health Zone

Clinic, organ, fertility, immune, prosthetic, body-subscription, and pathogen-control district.

Mechanics:

  • Champion healing/upgrades;
  • class-restricted survival;
  • body-rights scandals;
  • quarantine events;
  • clinic seizure/defense;
  • debt and repossession stakes.

Meridian Station Zone

White Sky maintenance node and dependency economy.

Mechanics:

  • Climate Stability;
  • Sensor Truth;
  • Albedo Capacity;
  • sabotage/exposure choices;
  • emergency powers;
  • Blue Break risk.

Free-Net / Exclusion Commons

Abandoned, informal, insurgent, data-corrupt, uninsurable, mesh-networked, or disaster-scarred territory.

Mechanics:

  • hidden paths;
  • unreliable intel;
  • archive access;
  • outlaw clinics;
  • local Champions;
  • low formal income;
  • resistance recruitment;
  • high myth/rumor density.

Backwater / Local Autonomy Zone

Low-value, remote, hard-to-model, or locally resistant territory outside strong corporate/state control.

Mechanics:

  • low income;
  • low surveillance;
  • local culture;
  • irregular recruitment;
  • hidden resources;
  • strange Digital-Net phenomena;
  • fragile independence.

Use a focused set for early design. Avoid simulating everything at once.

Recommended Prototype Resources

ComputeVI tools, climate models, drone coordination, provenance, Echo/Champion systems.Scouting, proof checks, cyber actions, automation.
LogisticsMovement, supply, evacuation, corridor control, repair capacity.Army sustainment, site upkeep, relief, trade.

Later / Optional Resources

  • Water / Cooling;
  • Food / Nutrients;
  • Attention;
  • Biotech / Bodyware;
  • Precursor Supply;
  • Cooling Credits;
  • Migration Permits;
  • Sensor Truth.

Recommendation:

Water/Cooling and Food should probably begin as region pressures or clocks unless the first prototype needs them as explicit resources.

Region Control States

A region or site should not simply be owned/unowned.

Control StateMeaningTypical Effects
State-administeredFormal public governance still controls daily services.Higher public legitimacy, slower response, weaker special outputs.
Corporate concessionService corporation controls key infrastructure under contract.Higher output/stability, higher dependency/surveillance.
ReceivershipEmergency financial/service control after crisis or bankruptcy.Stabilizes core services, erodes sovereignty.
Joint charter / UNP-supervisedMulti-actor oversight under treaty or emergency compact.Lower faction ownership, higher legal complexity.
Local autonomousLocal councils/militias/customs control daily life.Lower formal output, stronger irregular recruitment.
Free-Net influencedInformal networks and archives shape governance.Better leaks/routes, weaker admissibility, higher contamination.
Occupied / besiegedArmed force dominates or surrounds the region.Security high, legitimacy low, hazard upkeep fragile.
ContestedControl unresolved.Volatile events, overlapping outputs, crisis acceleration.
Collapsed / sacrificedSystems failed or were intentionally withdrawn.High hazard, high unrest, low formal economy, hidden potential.
Rogue-maintainedBlack-market or unauthorized actors keep systems running.Useful but unstable, illegal, and politically explosive.

Each state should affect:

  • income;
  • recruitment;
  • unrest;
  • hazard-clock speed;
  • access to sites;
  • private-security presence;
  • Champion legitimacy;
  • event table;
  • upkeep burden.

Crisis Clocks

Each scenario should foreground three active clocks. A full campaign may track more globally, but moment-to-moment play should stay readable.

Climate Hazard Clock

Heat rebound, flood, disease, crop failure, salinity, storm, monsoon distortion, or wet-bulb danger.

Slowed by:

  • Meridian operation;
  • adaptation infrastructure;
  • evacuation;
  • repair;
  • climate stabilization.

Accelerated by:

  • sabotage;
  • under-maintenance;
  • over-tuning;
  • corridor damage;
  • station failure;
  • denial of hazard data.

Legitimacy Clock

Public belief that current rulers, operators, or rebels have the right to act.

Slowed by:

  • relief;
  • fair access;
  • proof;
  • transparent action;
  • Champion mediation.

Accelerated by:

  • scandal;
  • repression;
  • visible inequality;
  • failed services;
  • feed panic;
  • body repossession.

Attribution Clock

Race to determine what caused harm before a legal/public narrative hardens.

Advanced by:

  • evidence missions;
  • sensor fortress raids;
  • Axiom certification;
  • Free-Net leaks;
  • Chorus campaigns;
  • witness protection.

If ignored, an enemy narrative becomes the default record.

Unrest / Revolt Clock

Labor strikes, riots, militia formation, migration surges, body-rights uprisings, or anti-sky mobilization.

Can be:

  • suppressed;
  • negotiated;
  • redirected;
  • legitimized;
  • allowed to become revolution.

Infrastructure Failure Clock

Pumps, seawalls, ports, datacenters, clinics, grids, roads, ledgers, or White Sky station subsystems.

Tied to:

  • upkeep;
  • corridor control;
  • energy;
  • compute;
  • crews;
  • legitimacy;
  • sabotage.

Corporate Emergency Powers Clock

Corporate/state operators use crisis to expand authority.

If it fills:

  • stability may improve;
  • surveillance increases;
  • contracts harden;
  • public authority shrinks;
  • opposition becomes illegal.

Free-Net Contamination / Liberation Clock

Archives, leaks, rumors, illegal identity systems, polluted feeds, and unverified truth spread.

Can reveal suppressed harm or collapse trust entirely.

Champion Myth Clock

A Champion’s public meaning escalates.

Possible endpoints:

  • defender;
  • fraud;
  • terrorist;
  • saint;
  • sovereign;
  • martyr;
  • corporate weapon;
  • Echo-haunted miracle;
  • failed human symbol.

Victory Types

Avoid “capture all towns” as the only structure.

Candidate victory types:

  • stabilize the district;
  • expose the cause;
  • seize the corridor;
  • defend or evacuate sacrificed populations;
  • force receivership renegotiation;
  • nationalize a station;
  • create a joint charter;
  • protect a work-town uprising;
  • suppress a revolt before infrastructure fails;
  • prevent a Blue Break cascade;
  • deliberately trigger a Blue Break and survive fallout;
  • make one faction’s explanation legally dominant;
  • keep a Champion alive long enough to testify;
  • let a Champion become a martyr to shift the campaign.

Campaign Structure

Recommended structure: braided crisis, not one linear apocalypse.

Act I: The Blue Break

Blue Week begins as scattered visible sky failures.

Design goals:

  • teach that blue sky is both wonder and threat;
  • introduce maintenance/sacrifice/contest status;
  • show Meridian Stations as life support and occupation;
  • introduce misinformation/fog-of-war through contradictory feeds.

Act II: The Attribution War

West African / Sahel / Gulf of Guinea rainfall claimants argue leaked tuning records prove harm.

Design goals:

  • make proof a resource;
  • fight over sensor sites, archives, tribunals, and public legitimacy;
  • show Axiom, Chorus, Free-Net, QXZ, states, and local movements pulling the same event into different truths.

Act III: Corridor Lockdown

Receiver cities, ports, cloud zones, migration work-towns, and Meridian Stations militarize.

Design goals:

  • open the HoMM3-style contest over sites, resources, corridors, and Champions;
  • force tradeoffs between stability, freedom, truth, and survival;
  • make logistics and maintenance as important as combat.

Act IV: The Human Cost

Body-rights, labor, migration, clinic, and cooling access crises bring the abstract climate war down to people.

Design goals:

  • turn body subscriptions and work-town contracts into objectives;
  • let Champions become mediators, symbols, or scandals;
  • make “saved infrastructure” morally incomplete if people remain owned by it.

Act V: Governance Settlement

The endgame is not simply “destroy or save White Sky.”

Possible settlement axes:

  • who controls maintenance;
  • who gets compensated;
  • who gets sacrificed;
  • what truths become admissible;
  • how corporations are constrained or empowered;
  • whether regions gain albedo sovereignty;
  • whether Champions remain people or become institutions.

Possible ending directions:

  • corporate stabilization victory;
  • public oversight / UNP charter victory;
  • regional albedo sovereignty;
  • atmospheric commons;
  • Blue Restorationist rupture;
  • Free-Net truth victory that destabilizes everything;
  • fragmented local maintenance regimes;
  • hybrid compromise where no faction fully wins.

Recommended First Playable Campaign Region

Best Current Recommendation: Lagos / Gulf of Guinea Receiver-Port Corridor with Inland Rainfall Attribution Links

Why this is strong:

  • recognizable real geography;
  • coastal flooding and port continuity;
  • cable/logistics/provenance stakes;
  • receiver-city politics;
  • White Sky propaganda versus lived sea-level harm;
  • inland Sahel rainfall-claim connection;
  • migration work-town pressure;
  • nearby Free-Net / sacrificed flood districts;
  • possible offshore marine cloud-brightening hub or Meridian node;
  • urban density plus regional strategic corridors;
  • strong agency for local states, activists, workers, corporations, Free-Net archivists, and Champions.

Possible package:

  • Lagos Lagoon Receiver Districts;
  • Gulf of Guinea Coastal Protection Authorities;
  • Port Continuity Corridor;
  • nearby Bonded Cloud / Data Entrepôt;
  • migration work-town belt;
  • inland Rainfall Compensation District link;
  • offshore Meridian or cloud-brightening hub;
  • Free-Net archive in sacrificed flood districts;
  • Axiom proof dispute over casualty/weather records;
  • Chorus narrative war over Blue Break footage.

Blue Tear Over the Port

A receiver-port corridor sees real blue sky above a floodline district.

Objectives:

  • stabilize station output;
  • seize the port;
  • evacuate flood districts;
  • amplify or suppress unrest;
  • prove whether the Blue Break was sabotage, negligence, or rogue tuning.

Clocks:

  • Climate Hazard;
  • Market Panic / Legitimacy;
  • Attribution.

A tribunal convoy carries sensor records that may prove White Sky altered rainfall timing.

Objectives:

  • protect the convoy;
  • raid it;
  • forge or leak records;

  • amplify testimony through Chorus.

  • Attribution;
  • Unrest;

  • suppress strike;

  • Unrest;

Objectives:

  • storm the station;

Risks

Too Many Layers

Risk: the map becomes incomprehensible.

Mitigation: each scenario foregrounds two or three layers; the rest become modifiers or unlock later.

Moral Flattening

Risk: corporations become pure villains and rebels pure heroes.

Mitigation: maintained systems save lives; sabotage can kill; rebels may be right and reckless; corporate operators may be sincere and coercive.

Africa as Victim-Zone

Risk: West Africa / Sahel becomes a passive suffering backdrop.

Mitigation: emphasize local states, activists, courts, youth movements, urban power, culture, corporate collaborators, Free-Net institutions, Champions, and internal disagreements.

White Sky Science Over Gameplay

Risk: climate modeling overwhelms the player.

Mitigation: convert science into readable clocks, overlays, disputed claims, and specific objectives.

Global Scale Too Early

Risk: campaign simulates the entire planet before local stakes work.

Mitigation: start with one receiver-port/rainfall region and imply global echoes through events and diplomacy.

Maintenance Binary

Risk: maintained good / sacrificed bad.

Mitigation: maintained means controlled; sacrificed means abandoned but alive; contested means possibility.

Sabotage as Automatically Evil

Risk: choices become obvious if sabotage always causes mass death.

Mitigation: distinguish exposure, selective disruption, spoofing, redundancy discovery, delayed clocks, and evacuation preparation.

Lore-Only Drift

Risk: bridge becomes a world essay instead of design material.

Mitigation: always translate back into map objects, resources, clocks, hero roles, victory conditions, and UI layers.

Decisions Needed

  1. What is the first campaign region?

    • Recommendation: Lagos / Gulf of Guinea receiver-port corridor with inland rainfall attribution links.

  2. What is the default visible map layer?

    • Recommendation: maintenance status first, with national borders visible underneath.

  3. How many resources in the prototype?

    • Recommendation: Energy, Compute, Logistics, Legitimacy, Proof, Climate Stability.

  4. How many crisis clocks per scenario?

    • Recommendation: three active clocks.

  5. Is Blue Week global, regional, or scattered?

    • Recommendation: scattered global phenomenon; first campaign studies one regional consequence.

  6. What caused Blue Week?

    • Recommendation: eco-anarchist / Open Sky action is real, but it triggers or reveals other failures. Several explanations can be partly true.

  7. Are Meridian Stations capturable map sites?

    • Recommendation: yes, but not all by combat. Some are treaty, exposure, engineering, or legitimacy objectives.

  8. Can the player intentionally trigger Blue Breaks?

    • Recommendation: eventually yes, but early campaign should make this dangerous and politically costly.

  9. Are corporate-aligned stabilizers playable?

    • Recommendation: yes or at least semi-playable. Without this, the central moral dilemma weakens.

  10. Are states meaningful actors or scenery?

    • Recommendation: meaningful but constrained. States provide legitimacy, armies, courts, identity, and national myths; corporations provide continuity services; neither fully rules alone.

  11. Can sacrificed territories be improved?

    • Recommendation: yes, but improvement requires choosing a maintenance model, not just spending resources.

  12. What does winning mean?

    • Recommendation: forcing a governance settlement under crisis, not saving the world absolutely.
