Player Fantasy

Lead a Champion-led faction across an Arctic cyberpunk wilderness where every outpost, convoy, fish plant, sensor tower, mine, data relay, and climate station can decide who owns the future under the White Sky.

The fantasy is not “conquer a wasteland.” It is:

  • outmaneuver rival factions across hostile terrain;
  • seize strategic infrastructure;
  • expose or bury truth;
  • upgrade assets with stolen Intel;
  • make public victories matter;
  • survive a PR war where climate, sovereignty, and survival are all monetized.

Campaign Premise

Blue Week is now treated as both summit and sky-break event.

The “summit” means a public Arctic development week in Greenland: investment expo, climate-adaptation showcase, mineral-rights negotiation, UNP Net-security exercise, local autonomy finance forum, and White Sky maintenance demonstration. It is Davos, COP, arms fair, infrastructure auction, and corporate PR pilgrimage compressed into one week.

Then the name becomes literal: part of the sky opens.

Some people see real blue. Some feeds show nothing. Some climate models deny the event. Some corporate layers show curated blue. Some dark-net channels claim the White Sky node was sabotaged. Champions are already there as public faces and deniable operators, so the summit turns into a strategic campaign.

Core Resources

Prototype resource set for this campaign:

  • Credits: payments, procurement, bribes, contracts.
  • Materials: construction, repairs, fortification.
  • Energy: power, heat, cooling, convoy logistics, compute support.
  • Compute: VI, Net actions, drones, prediction, hacking.
  • Biotech: body adaptation, clinics, wildlife control, elite units.
  • Legitimacy / Proof: legal/public permission, local support, Axiom-admissible evidence.
  • Intel: special upgrade resource for Champion assets, hacks, asset sets, and hidden map knowledge.

Intel System Bridge

Intel should be modeled on Olden Era’s Alchemical Dust pattern: a scarce cross-system upgrade resource acquired from the map, conversion structures, dismantling inventory, and hero/skill specializations; spent on town/recruitment upgrades, spell-equivalent upgrades, and artifact/asset empowerment.

Empower artifactsupgrade Champion assets, bodyware, drone systems, neural decks, seals, and blackmail archives

Intel is gained through:

  • data heists;
  • captured proof nodes;
  • defeated rival Champions;
  • environmental-model leaks;
  • dark-net caches;
  • local knowledge agreements;
  • reverse-engineering assets;
  • infiltrating corporate grids;
  • public scandal wins;
  • recurring Analysis Cell or specialist production.

It is spent on:

  • upgrading assets/artifacts;
  • upgrading recruitment sites / unit lines;
  • leveling hacks, doctrines, and operations;
  • unlocking set bonuses;
  • revealing hidden map layers;
  • cleaning polluted information;
  • legal/provenance strikes.

Guardrail: Intel should feel like secrets turned into power, not blue mana. Avoid making it a mandatory tax on every basic building.

Strategic Map Layer

Physical layer:

  • settlements;
  • ports;
  • airstrips;
  • fjords;
  • mines;
  • fish plants;
  • ice routes;
  • weather stations;
  • White Sky node sites;
  • hydro plants;
  • convoy routes.

Digital-Net layer:

  • Treaty Net blue lanes;
  • corporate grids;
  • Free-Net relays;
  • dark-net caches;
  • proof nodes;
  • spoofed sensors;
  • AGI quarantine chokepoints;
  • ghost servers under legacy infrastructure.

Switching layers should change what the player sees and what actions are possible, not become a separate minigame disconnected from map control.

Town / Outpost Types

  • Capital Hub: politics, media, contracts, diplomacy, elite recruitment.
  • Port / Fishery Hub: credits, food/logistics, convoy access, local labor.
  • Air / SAR Hub: mobility, rescue, rapid deployment, emergency events.
  • Mine Camp: rare resources, pollution risk, protest risk, security pressure.
  • Data / Sensor Station: scouting, proof, Net access, White Sky calibration.
  • Clinic / Body Shop: Champion healing, biotech units, adaptation mods.
  • Free-Net Commons: Intel, leaks, irregular recruits, misinformation risk.
  • Wildlife / Patrol Station: terrain mastery, animal-control units, local guides.

Possible Factions in the First Campaign

Recommended campaign faction set:

1. Home Rule Coalition

Local-government, municipal, hunter/fisher, labor, and autonomy coalition. They are not anti-development; they want development that does not turn sovereignty into a subscription.

Gameplay angle: legitimacy, local routes, SAR, guides, fisheries, consent, defensive terrain, lower-tech but high-map-knowledge units.

Why include: keeps Greenland from becoming scenery and gives the player an emotionally grounded faction.

2. QXZ Meridian Arctic Mandate

White Sky operator and climate-liability machine. It insists the Greenland node is life-support infrastructure and that sabotage risks mass death.

Gameplay angle: climate stations, hazard mitigation, elite infrastructure security, weather control-lite effects, high legitimacy with institutions but low trust locally.

Why include: makes White Sky playable and morally unstable.

3. Barents Research Group / Polar Certification Combine

Polar data, route certification, extraction-risk scoring, rescue/security, sensor fleets, and “neutral” scientific authority that quietly decides who can operate in the Arctic.

Gameplay angle: scouting, route control, sensor truth, drones, ice-risk manipulation, mercantile resource flow.

Why include: very cyberpunk, less obvious than a generic mining corp, strong fit for Greenland.

4. Digital Peacekeeping Directorate — “the Blue” / “the Blues”

UNP cyber peacekeepers responsible for Treaty Net blue lanes, emergency civic access, AGI quarantine, and corporate gateway audits.

Gameplay angle: defensive Net control, proof stabilization, emergency access, anti-hack tools, diplomatic constraints, mandate-based powers.

Why include: realizes the UNP Net idea and gives a non-corporate institutional faction that is neither pure good nor villain.

5. Open Sky / Blue Week Cells

Atmospheric commons activists, direct-action networks, unceiling hackers, climate-justice lawyers, and sabotage cells. Not anti-science; anti-proprietary survival hostage system.

Gameplay angle: sabotage, Free-Net leaks, morale/public shock, cheap irregulars, risky high-impact climate/provenance actions.

Why include: makes Blue Week ideologically alive and not just an accident.

6. Janus-Kestrel / Mining-Logistics Consortium

Green-transition extraction, ports, finance, cargo identity, private security, and infrastructure investment. It promises jobs and independence capital while binding the island to concession contracts.

Gameplay angle: money, construction tempo, contracts, private security, logistics, resource extraction, debt pressure.

Why include: gives the “development dollars” fight a concrete player.

7. Tundra Biosecurity / Wildlife Control Program

Could be a subfaction, neutral dwelling chain, or full faction later. It weaponizes climate-stressed wildlife management, telemetry collars, bioacoustic herding, and animal sensor networks.

Gameplay angle: polar bears, ravens, musk oxen, sled logistics, biosecurity traps, wilderness control.

Why include: preserves the cool polar-animal fantasy without breaking no-magic rules.

Recommended first playable pair for MVP: Home Rule Coalition vs QXZ Meridian. Recommended third/fourth for vertical slice: Barents Research Group and the Blues.

Animal-Control Hook

This can be a faction feature if grounded as:

  • hacked wildlife telemetry;
  • bioacoustic herding;
  • neuro-interface collars;
  • climate-stress disease monitoring turned military;
  • drone/animal hybrid sensor network.

Example units:

  • Polar Bear Bio-Tag Alpha: rare, terrifying, ethically radioactive.
  • Musk Ox Stampede Rig: area-control unit.
  • Raven Sensor Swarm: scouting and signal spoofing.
  • Sled Dog Logistics Team: movement/rescue bonus.
  • Seal / Narwhal Buoy Network: coastal detection and convoy routing.

Victory Conditions

Possible scenario goals:

  • secure the Pituffik Meridian Node without causing termination-risk panic;
  • win enough local consent/proof to legitimize a development package;
  • expose the falsified Blue Week model before a rival controls the narrative;
  • evacuate settlements during a White Sky / weather failure while holding key sites;
  • capture or destroy rival Intel vaults;
  • keep the Treaty Net blue lanes open through the crisis;
  • trigger or prevent a larger Blue Break.

MVP Slice

Smallest playable test:

  • one Greenland region map;
  • three hubs;
  • two Champions/factions;
  • four resources: Credits, Energy, Proof, Intel;
  • one physical map layer and one simple Digital-Net overlay;
  • asset upgrade using Intel;
  • one Blue Week crisis clock;
  • 8-12 map sites;
  • one full tactical battle prototype for guarded sites.

Risks

RiskMitigation
Greenland becomes exotic sceneryMake local consent, sovereignty, and municipal survival mechanically meaningful.
“Green land” becomes implausibleKeep agriculture limited, high-tech, symbolic, and contested.
UNP Net authority cleans up cyberpunkMake it partial, treaty-bound, underfunded, and politically compromised.
Intel becomes generic manaTie acquisition and spending to secrets, proof, and research.
Animal control becomes sillyGround it in telemetry, biosecurity, climate adaptation, and coercive wildlife management.
Too many resourcesPrototype with four, expand only after fun is proven.
HoMM inspiration becomes reskinPreserve strategic clarity, movement constraints, guarded sites, hubs, recruitment, and map-object readability.

Decision Needed

  • Name and legal framing of the campaign region.
  • Official name of the UNP Net-security authority.
  • First playable factions.
  • Whether Blue Week is initially caused by eco-anarchists, corporate false flag, maintenance failure, or deliberately ambiguous multi-cause crisis.
  • Exact MVP resource list.
