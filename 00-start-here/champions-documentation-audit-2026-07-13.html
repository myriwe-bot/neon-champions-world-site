Champions Documentation Audit — 2026-07-13

Scope

Compared available Hermes session history with the current world vault and game-design repository to determine whether major Champion decisions remain chat-only.

Key history windows checked:

  • 2026-05-15: initial Champion ontology, social ambiguity, faction-relative recognition, Cyberpunk-style legends, transhumanist/strong-leader myth, and parasocial worship.
  • 2026-05-18: Champions as a human-exceptionalist answer to AI anxiety.
  • 2026-05-22: Champions as public faces, deniable operators, strategic hero-commanders, and users of Intel-upgraded Assets.
  • 2026-05-28 onward: off-board commander assumption; Marshal/Operator poles; Command, Operations, Doctrine, primary stats, progression, recruitment, defeat, Assets, and secondary skills.
  • 2026-07-10: Claude’s July 6 plan containing faction-specific Champion seeds for HRC, QXZ, Barents, and an obsolete war-OS version of Janus-Kestrel.
  • 2026-07-13: Champions as the coalition gravity that can make a loose HRC protection network operate as one faction.

Verdict

CONCERNS — mostly recorded, but fragmented

The major Champion ideas were not lost. The current documents preserve substantially more than the chat-level summaries alone suggest. The problem is fragmentation and authority clarity rather than missing bulk content.

Well Covered

World ontology and social meaning

canon/world-rules/champions.md records:

  • Championhood as partly condition, role, title, reputation, recognition, and myth;
  • no universal biological definition or required superpower;
  • faction-relative, popular, battlefield, technological, self-claimed, and refused recognition;
  • corporate assets, legends, commanders, rebels, saints, frauds, and ordinary people elevated through deeds;
  • transhumanist/übermensch and strong-leader mythology;
  • social love, fear, loathing, exploitation, envy, and worship;
  • possible origin in human-exceptionalist reaction to AI displacement.

cultures/champion-worship.md records the parasocial, celebrity, religious, political, and Chorus-mediated layer.

Game-facing role

design-bridges/champions-design-bridge.md records:

  • Champions as HoMM-style strategic hero-commanders rather than generic superheroes;
  • army, faction, territory, reputation, body modification, and story integration;
  • recognition tiers, false/failed/illegal/refused Champions;
  • progression, dependency, Assets, resource hooks, faction signaling, and campaign consequences.

Detailed systems

The game-design GDD design/gdd/tactical-combat/champion-operations-and-progression.md records approved or working decisions for:

  • off-board command role;
  • Marshal/Operator archetype poles;
  • Command, Operations, and Doctrine;
  • primary stats and progression;
  • Operation channels, preparation, resistance, and counterplay;
  • Champion build grammar and unit specialization;
  • curated/rotating faction and neutral recruitment;
  • HoMM-like defeat as loss of control rather than routine permanent death;
  • personal and command-layer Assets;
  • Intel upgrades and duplicate dismantling;
  • secondary skill pool, ranks, offer rules, and starting skills.

Gaps Found and Repaired

1. Why factions cohere around Champions

The documents listed many reasons troops might follow Champions, but did not clearly state that unstable factions can form around them rather than merely employ them.

Repair: added Faction Gravity and Command to canon/world-rules/champions.md. It preserves multiple command mechanisms and records HRC as the first explicit case of Champion-mediated coalition formation.

2. HRC-specific Champion relationship

The former HRC page treated Champions as an open question and described the coalition more like a stable local faction.

Repair: revised organizations/home-rule-coalition.md so the pre-crisis network exists without unified command, while different Champions pull different institutions, activists, workers, volunteers, and defectors into temporary operational coalitions.

3. Discovery and source hierarchy

World ontology, cultural meaning, design bridge, and detailed mechanics live in separate artifacts without a single status map.

Current reading order:

  1. canon/world-rules/champions.md — what Championhood means in-world; soft-canon seed.
  2. cultures/champion-worship.md — public myth and parasocial culture; soft-canon seed.
  3. design-bridges/champions-design-bridge.md — translation into strategic hero/commander functions; game bridge.
  4. Game-design design/gdd/tactical-combat/champion-operations-and-progression.md — detailed system decisions and working contracts.

4. Named candidate discovery

The first pass correctly found the system and ontology material but missed faction-specific seeds embedded inside the long Claude plan pasted on 2026-07-10. Those concepts were not present in current named-character authority.

Repair: recovered and preserved them without canon promotion in raw/brainstorms/claude-champion-seeds-2026-07-06.md. The note separates reusable character cores from rejected Protocol terminology, obsolete faction roles, and protagonist/antagonist framing.

Important Unresolved Questions

These should remain open rather than being silently inferred from mechanics:

  • Is every player-controlled hero a Champion, or can ordinary officers command armies?
  • How many Champions can a faction field, recognize, or sustain at once?
  • Which parts of Champion distinction are materially measurable, and which exist only through recognition?
  • How much personal battlefield presence should individual Champions have outside the current off-board tactical baseline?
  • What is the first campaign’s named HRC Champion, and which coalition currents recognize them?
  • How does QXZ manufacture, sponsor, insure, audit, or legally own its Champions?
  • Which Champion mechanics are prototype commitments versus later product scope?

Conclusion

No wholesale Champion rewrite is needed. The central ontology, social myth, strategic role, and detailed mechanical vocabulary are already documented. Future work should focus on named Champions, faction-specific relationships, player-facing clarity, and resolving a few high-level authority questions—not recreating the system from memory.
