Nation-States and Real-World Continuity — 2052

Purpose

This page preserves a corrective brainstorming pass: Neon Champions should not replace the world map with only abstract region labels like “Rainfall Compensation District” or “Bonded Cloud Zone.”

Those labels remain useful, but they should sit on top of recognizable countries, cities, rivers, coasts, ports, and cultural regions. The world should feel like our world was stressed, mortgaged, repaired, partitioned, and partially overlaid — not like a fantasy map with cyberpunk terms.

Core Correction

Most current nation-states should still exist in 2052.

Reasons:

  • states are sticky institutions;
  • passports, militaries, courts, languages, school systems, debts, and national myths persist;
  • corporations usually prefer states to remain formally responsible for unrest, welfare, policing, and debt;
  • citizens still organize politically through national identity;
  • international law still needs signatories;
  • climate compensation, albedo liability, migration, water, and resource disputes require state actors.

The world should not be “post-national.” It should be post-clean-sovereignty.

Recommended rule:

The official map looks mostly familiar. The lived map does not.

How to Use Region Labels

Earlier terms like maintained territories, sacrificed zones, compute concessions, coastal protection authorities, rainfall claims boards, and receiver districts should be overlays, not replacements.

Example:

  • Nigeria still exists.
  • Lagos still exists.
  • But parts of Lagos may be inside a Lagos Lagoon Receiver District, a Gulf of Guinea Coastal Protection Authority, a port continuity corridor, a corporate flood-defense concession, a feed-provenance jurisdiction, and several insurer redlines at the same time.

That is stronger than renaming Nigeria into an invented cyberpunk bloc.

State Continuity Categories

1. Strong Continuity States

Countries whose institutions remain recognizable, even if strained.

Likely examples:

  • United States
  • Canada
  • United Kingdom
  • France
  • Germany
  • Poland
  • Japan
  • South Korea
  • Australia
  • New Zealand
  • India
  • China
  • Turkey
  • Iran
  • Saudi Arabia
  • Egypt
  • Indonesia
  • Brazil
  • Mexico
  • South Africa

These states may have emergency laws, corporate concessions, climate migration politics, internal secessionist pressures, or privatized infrastructure, but they still appear as countries on official maps.

Use them when you need:

  • diplomacy;
  • armies;
  • sanctions;
  • intelligence services;
  • climate courts;
  • White Sky treaty politics;
  • national myth and propaganda.

2. Federal / Fragmented Continuity States

Countries that still exist legally, but feel internally uneven because provinces, states, autonomous regions, city-regions, private concessions, or emergency authorities matter as much as the capital.

Likely examples:

  • United States
  • India
  • Nigeria
  • Brazil
  • Mexico
  • Indonesia
  • Pakistan
  • Democratic Republic of the Congo
  • Ethiopia
  • South Africa
  • Russia
  • China

These are excellent for Neon Champions because they allow recognizable states plus local variation.

Examples:

  • The United States remains the United States, but California water/compute law, Gulf storm insurance, interior heat corridors, and Arctic logistics politics create very different lived sovereignties.
  • India remains India, but heat law, monsoon politics, water compacts, coastal risk, platform identity, and state-level industrial corridors create strong internal map layers.
  • Nigeria remains Nigeria, but Lagos, the Niger Delta, northern water/security zones, and mineral/logistics corridors feel like different political worlds.

3. Receiver / Concession States

Countries that remain internationally recognized but have ceded major infrastructure, budgetary, port, water, health, or energy functions to corporate consortia, creditors, or climate-rescue authorities.

This should be common in climate-bankrupted economies, but not only in poor countries. Rich cities and regions can also enter receivership.

Likely forms:

  • port concessions;
  • flood-defense concessions;
  • bonded cloud zones;
  • water/desalination concessions;
  • clinic and body-subscription systems;
  • migration work-town compacts;
  • resource corridor security agreements;
  • emergency debt receiverships.

Important nuance:

These are not necessarily “countries bought by corporations” in a cartoon sense. Often the public story is rescue, stabilization, modernization, climate adaptation, or credit restoration.

4. Patchwork / Partial Failure States

Countries whose central government remains on maps and at the UN-equivalent, but where large territories are governed by a mix of local authorities, militias, NGOs, private security, religious networks, city governments, Free-Net systems, and concession companies.

Possible candidates:

  • Libya
  • Yemen
  • Syria
  • Sudan / South Sudan area
  • parts of the Sahel
  • parts of Myanmar
  • parts of Haiti
  • parts of Afghanistan
  • parts of the DRC
  • parts of the Amazon frontier

Use carefully. Avoid turning real places into generic chaos zones. The interesting version has institutions, not absence of institutions.

5. Microstate / City-State Survivors

Small states can become disproportionately important if they control finance, ports, arbitration, data law, orbital launch, cables, or climate courts.

Likely examples:

  • Singapore
  • UAE city-emirates, especially Dubai and Abu Dhabi
  • Qatar
  • Bahrain
  • Luxembourg
  • Switzerland
  • Monaco
  • Panama
  • Rwanda as a compact governance / data / logistics actor, if chosen
  • island states as climate-law plaintiffs and maritime-rights actors

These should be powerful not because they are huge, but because they host treaties, arbitration, model registries, financial instruments, ports, compute, or climate-liability courts.

6. Displaced / Virtualized States

Some low-lying island states, heavily flooded deltas, or depopulated territories may retain legal personhood even after large-scale displacement.

Possible forms:

  • governments-in-exile;
  • maritime-rights litigation states;
  • diaspora citizenship platforms;
  • cloud embassies;
  • treaty-recognized digital civil registries;
  • climate-compensation claimants;
  • floating or leased administrative districts.

This is a good place to use Digital-Net and identity themes without making everything abstract.

Countries That Probably Still Exist

A useful default: almost all UN-recognized states still exist unless there is a strong story reason otherwise.

Do not casually erase countries. If a country disappears, it should matter.

Better defaults:

  • survives but is poorer;
  • survives but has ceded infrastructure;
  • survives but lost coastal districts;
  • survives but gained migrants;
  • survives but has autonomous regions;
  • survives but is locked into a climate treaty;
  • survives but is internally split between maintained, sacrificed, and contested territories.

Countries / Borders That Might Change

Use sparingly. Border changes should come from specific pressures.

Sea-Level and Delta Changes

Possible affected areas:

  • Bangladesh / Bay of Bengal;
  • Nile Delta / Egypt;
  • Mekong Delta / Vietnam;
  • Mississippi Delta / United States;
  • Netherlands / Rhine-Meuse-Scheldt delta;
  • Lagos Lagoon / Gulf of Guinea;
  • Jakarta / Java coast;
  • Shanghai / Yangtze Delta;
  • Pearl River Delta;
  • small island states.

Likely political result:

Not full disappearance, but protected cores, abandoned belts, relocation zones, new port authorities, salinity borders, and litigation over who pays.

Water-Basin Politics

Possible flashpoints:

  • Nile Basin;
  • Indus Basin;
  • Ganges-Brahmaputra-Meghna Basin;
  • Mekong Basin;
  • Tigris-Euphrates;
  • Colorado River;
  • La Plata / Parana;
  • Zambezi;
  • Lake Chad;
  • Central Asian water systems.

Likely political result:

More compacts, tribunals, emergency releases, sabotage, and basin authorities — not necessarily new nation-states.

Arctic / Subarctic Shift

Possible affected states:

  • Russia;
  • Canada;
  • United States via Alaska;
  • Norway;
  • Denmark/Greenland;
  • Iceland;
  • Finland;
  • Sweden.

Likely political result:

Not a clean new Arctic empire. Instead: rescue contracts, insurance-approved routes, indigenous sovereignty disputes, mining concessions, naval incidents, cable routes, cold datacenters, and ports that are strategic for only part of the year.

Corporate-State Purchases / Receiverships

Most plausible in:

  • climate-bankrupt cities;
  • port states;
  • mineral-export corridors;
  • small indebted states;
  • special economic zones;
  • island states trading legal jurisdiction for survival financing;
  • disaster-hit rich-city districts that need flood/cooling bonds.

This does not require the country to vanish. The corporate zone can sit inside it.

Secession / Autonomy Movements

Use only where it adds story.

Possible categories:

  • resource-rich regions resisting capital-city debt deals;
  • cities wanting climate autonomy;
  • indigenous Arctic/subarctic governance;
  • delta regions demanding relocation/compensation authority;
  • regions refusing White Sky fees or albedo treaties;
  • Free-Net-aligned territories rejecting platform identity.

Recommended Global State Shape

North America

United States

Still exists. Probably more internally uneven than collapsed.

Likely features:

  • federal emergency climate and compute law;
  • state-level divergence;
  • Gulf Coast storm/insurance retreat;
  • western water compacts and fire insurance zones;
  • Great Lakes climate-migration politics;
  • Alaska/Arctic logistics importance;
  • corporate receiverships in some cities or infrastructure corridors;
  • strong White Sky establishment faction in finance, defense, insurance, and climate services.

Good tension:

The United States claims to defend democracy against corporate sovereignty while outsourcing enormous parts of climate survival to corporate contractors.

Canada

Still exists and becomes more strategically important.

Likely features:

  • Arctic access;
  • freshwater politics;
  • mineral corridors;
  • wildfire displacement;
  • indigenous sovereignty conflicts and treaty resurgence;
  • cold datacenters;
  • migration pressure from south and abroad.

Canada can be a White Sky beneficiary and a contested Arctic actor at once.

Mexico

Still exists, likely pivotal.

Likely features:

  • heat and water stress;
  • manufacturing nearshoring / automated industry;
  • migration bottleneck and bargaining power;
  • Pacific and Gulf port corridors;
  • cartel-to-security-company transformations;
  • body-labor and clinic economies;
  • food and water politics.

Mexico should not be a passive border zone. It is a manufacturing, labor, migration, and logistics power.

Europe

Europe should still be recognizable, but politically colder, older, more technocratic, and more defensive.

European Union

Likely still exists in some form, but more treaty-stack than idealistic union.

Possible name if changed: European Continuity Union, European Climate Union, or simply still the EU.

Likely features:

  • climate courts;
  • strict feed provenance;
  • migration screening;
  • insurance-state politics;
  • high support for White Sky because heat deaths and crop shocks were reduced;
  • internal fights over southern water stress and northern adaptation costs;
  • corporate regulation that is real but often captured through compliance layers.

United Kingdom, France, Germany, Poland, Italy, Spain, Netherlands, Nordics

These mostly survive.

Interesting internal changes:

  • Netherlands becomes an archetypal maintained territory: technically brilliant, expensive, morally exposed because protection is selective.
  • Spain/Italy/Greece face heat, water, agriculture, tourism-collapse, and migration politics.
  • Nordics gain Arctic/logistics/mineral importance but suffer geopolitical pressure.
  • Germany/France remain industrial-regulatory powers but depend on external minerals, energy, and climate stability.
  • Poland and Eastern Europe can be defense/manufacturing/logistics frontiers.

Russia and Post-Soviet Space

Russia likely still exists, but weakened, militarized, and highly uneven.

Options:

  1. Continuity Russia: still a major state, paranoid, resource-heavy, Arctic-facing, sanctions-adapted.
  2. Patchwork Russia: formal state persists, but regions, oligarchic infrastructure authorities, military districts, and Arctic concessions matter more.
  3. Russian Federation plus breakaway resource compacts: more dramatic, but use carefully.

Likely features:

  • Arctic routes;
  • permafrost infrastructure damage;
  • minerals;
  • nuclear legacy;
  • military-industrial autonomy;
  • data/cable sabotage;
  • climate migration from Central Asia;
  • state-backed corporate zones.

Ukraine should likely still matter as a hardened reconstruction, agriculture, drone, cyber, and food-security state, unless later canon says otherwise.

Central Asia becomes important through water, uranium, minerals, logistics, and Chinese/Russian/Turkish/Gulf competition.

Middle East / North Africa

Do not flatten into desert collapse. This region is central to manufactured habitability.

Gulf States

Saudi Arabia, UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Oman, Bahrain probably still exist, but with transformed legitimacy.

Likely features:

  • desalination sovereignty;
  • sealed/cooling urbanism;
  • sovereign wealth converted into climate services;
  • migrant labor dependence;
  • solar resentment under White Sky;
  • AI/compute/cooling projects;
  • atmospheric-engineering finance;
  • religious and political debates over manufactured sky.

The Gulf can be both White Sky beneficiary and critic: cooling helps survival, but sky-brightening harms solar and makes them dependent on external climate tuning.

Iran and Turkey

Both likely persist as major regional powers.

Turkey:

  • water, migration, Black Sea, drones, logistics, NATO/Eurasian bargaining.

Iran:

  • heat, water, mountains, energy, sanctions adaptation, biotech/black-market science, regional networks.

Egypt

Still exists, but the Nile Delta and Nile water politics make it highly pressured.

Likely features:

  • Nile Basin climate attribution disputes;
  • Delta protection and relocation;
  • Cairo heat/cooling politics;
  • canal/security/logistics importance;
  • food import vulnerability.

Sub-Saharan Africa

Do not turn Africa into one bloc. Existing states mostly remain, but many contain intense overlays.

Nigeria

Must remain important.

Likely features:

  • Lagos/Gulf of Guinea coastal protection fights;
  • Niger Delta energy transition concessions;
  • northern rainfall/security politics;
  • youth movements;
  • platform economies;
  • megacity culture;
  • religious and climate-justice movements;
  • corporate and state competition.

Nigeria is a good center for White Sky opposition, but not uniformly anti-White-Sky.

Ghana, Cote d’Ivoire, Senegal

Useful as West African political, port, climate-law, and cultural actors.

Possible roles:

  • climate courts;
  • coastal adaptation;
  • port corridors;
  • youth media and protest;
  • rainfall compensation coalitions;
  • Free-Net publishing hubs.

Mali, Niger, Burkina Faso, Chad, Cameroon

Use carefully and specifically.

Likely features:

  • rainfall timing;
  • pastoralist/farmer route disputes;
  • mineral corridors;
  • security contractors;
  • Lake Chad pulse politics;
  • climate compensation claims;
  • religious and local governance networks.

Democratic Republic of the Congo

Extremely important.

Likely features:

  • cobalt/copper/mineral corridors;
  • Congo Basin carbon custody;
  • peat protection;
  • Kinshasa as legal/data/concession center;
  • conservation militarization;
  • bio-prospecting;
  • corporate/state/local conflict.

The contradiction: the world wants the Congo to be both a mine and a forest.

Ethiopia, Kenya, Tanzania, Rwanda, Uganda

East Africa should be varied.

Likely features:

  • highland climate refuge zones;
  • Nile/Horn water politics;
  • logistics corridors;
  • Indian Ocean ports;
  • geothermal energy;
  • data/identity governance;
  • food and migration politics.

South Africa

Still a major state, but strained.

Likely features:

  • grid politics;
  • mineral and industrial base;
  • inequality and private security;
  • water stress;
  • corporate enclaves;
  • southern African migration;
  • biotech/clinic economy.

South Asia

India

Still exists and should be one of the world-shaping powers.

Likely features:

  • heat law;
  • monsoon politics;
  • coastal risk;
  • platform identity;
  • massive internal variation;
  • pharma/biotech;
  • compute and chip ambitions;
  • water disputes;
  • democratic, corporate, and technocratic tensions.

India can be both White Sky beneficiary and critic depending on region and season.

Pakistan

Still exists, under water/heat/debt/security pressure.

Likely features:

  • Indus Basin politics;
  • heat emergencies;
  • military/civilian tension;
  • Chinese/Gulf/Western infrastructure finance;
  • migration;
  • agriculture and food shocks.

Bangladesh

Very important because of delta adaptation, migration, textiles/manufacturing, and climate law.

Do not erase it. Better: Bangladesh becomes a world expert in adaptation, relocation bureaucracy, and flood urbanism while also being exploited by climate finance.

Sri Lanka / Nepal / Bhutan / Maldives

Useful smaller actors.

  • Sri Lanka: ports, debt, Indian Ocean logistics.
  • Nepal/Bhutan: water, hydropower, Himalayan climate, India/China pressure.
  • Maldives: displaced/virtualized sovereignty, maritime rights, luxury-climate hypocrisy.

East Asia

China

Still exists. Likely one of the main White Sky, compute, manufacturing, and infrastructure powers.

Likely features:

  • coastal megacity protection;
  • interior water transfer;
  • state-corporate fused infrastructure;
  • controlled platform identity;
  • mineral security abroad;
  • climate-engineering legitimacy claims;
  • export of maintained-territory systems.

Good tension:

China argues that corporate extraterritoriality is Western hypocrisy while running its own state-corporate concession systems abroad.

Japan

Still exists, older, richer, technologically deep, disaster-adapted.

Likely features:

  • robotics;
  • coastal defense;
  • eldercare bodies;
  • feed provenance;
  • sea/food security;
  • corporate-state continuity.

Korea

South Korea likely remains a high-tech state. The Korean peninsula could be:

  1. still divided;
  2. confederated under extreme security/compute arrangements;
  3. partially collapsed/reconstructed in the North;
  4. reunified but financially traumatized.

Recommendation: keep division or ambiguous confederation unless a strong story needs reunification.

Taiwan

Very important due to chips, sea security, and geopolitical ambiguity.

Options:

  1. Taiwan remains de facto autonomous under hardened deterrence and distributed fabs.
  2. Taiwan crisis led to partial internationalization of chip sovereignty.
  3. A treaty created special semiconductor neutrality zones.

Do not casually resolve Taiwan unless it is central.

Southeast Asia

Existing states mostly remain: Indonesia, Vietnam, Thailand, Philippines, Malaysia, Singapore, Myanmar, Cambodia, Laos.

Likely features:

  • Mekong politics;
  • coastal and delta risk;
  • ports/cables;
  • manufacturing;
  • maritime security;
  • heat and storm adaptation;
  • corporate work towns;
  • Singapore as arbitration / compute / port / climate-finance hub.

Indonesia is especially important: huge population, islands, nickel, relocation from Jakarta, maritime chokepoints, Islamic politics, and climate adaptation.

Vietnam: Mekong Delta and manufacturing power.

Philippines: storms, labor export, maritime conflict, clinics, remittances, Free-Net culture.

Myanmar: likely patchwork unless later canon stabilizes it.

Latin America

Brazil

Still exists and should be pivotal.

Likely features:

  • Amazon sovereignty;
  • carbon finance;
  • agribusiness;
  • drought/flood cycles;
  • hydropower;
  • mineral and food exports;
  • coastal megacities;
  • corporate conservation conflict.

Brazil’s contradiction:

The world demands the Amazon be preserved, monetized, surveilled, farmed around, mined around, and used as climate collateral.

Argentina, Chile, Uruguay, Paraguay

Southern Cone remains important.

Likely features:

  • food;
  • lithium;
  • water;
  • Antarctic/subantarctic logistics;
  • climate migration;
  • relatively attractive temperate zones;
  • corporate agriculture and seed IP.

Chile: lithium, copper, ports, water politics.

Argentina: food, lithium, debt politics, Patagonian climate refuge speculation.

Peru, Bolivia, Ecuador, Colombia, Venezuela

Likely features:

  • Andes water/glacier loss;
  • lithium/minerals;
  • Amazon edge politics;
  • oil transition;
  • migration;
  • ports;
  • drug networks converting into security/logistics/data networks.

Venezuela can be a petrostate afterlife: oil infrastructure, migration, minerals, political fragmentation, climate debt.

Colombia can be a biodiversity/security/logistics state with strong city cultures.

Oceania and Pacific

Australia

Still exists, strategically important.

Likely features:

  • heat/fire adaptation;
  • minerals;
  • Indian/Pacific security;
  • controlled migration;
  • solar resentment under White Sky;
  • indigenous sovereignty;
  • coastal city protection;
  • remote datacenter/mining corridors.

New Zealand

Still exists, wealthy/defensive, possibly a refuge myth and biosecurity state.

Pacific Island States

Do not erase them. They become legally and morally important.

Likely forms:

  • partially displaced states;
  • maritime-rights litigants;
  • climate compensation leaders;
  • cloud civil registries;
  • leased land arrangements;
  • ocean monitoring and cable politics;
  • moral opposition to atmospheric intervention without consent.

Alliances and Blocs

Use real-world institutions as ancestors, not necessarily final forms.

White Sky Maintenance Coalition

Not necessarily a formal country bloc; more a treaty and infrastructure regime.

Likely members/supporters:

  • North Atlantic states;
  • parts of the EU;
  • China or Chinese-led infrastructure entities, depending on canon choice;
  • Japan/Korea/Taiwan-linked industrial actors;
  • Gulf climate-service financiers;
  • major insurers;
  • climate-engineering corporations;
  • cities saved by albedo cooling.

Atmospheric Sovereignty / Compensation Coalition

States and movements demanding liability, transparency, compensation, or regional vetoes over albedo tuning.

Likely participants:

  • West African states and civil coalitions;
  • Sahel basin actors;
  • Pacific island states;
  • solar-export states;
  • some Latin American rainforest states;
  • parts of South Asia;
  • climate justice NGOs;
  • Free-Net archivists.

Compute and Trusted Hardware Blocs

Could include:

  • US/EU/Japan/Korea/Taiwan networks;
  • China-centered networks;
  • Indian sovereign compute push;
  • Gulf-funded cloud enclaves;
  • neutral compute freeports;
  • black-market Free-Net compute.

Food / Nutrient Blocs

Food should matter as much as chips.

Likely actors:

  • United States;
  • Canada;
  • Brazil;
  • Argentina;
  • Ukraine;
  • Russia;
  • India;
  • Australia;
  • fertilizer states;
  • seed IP corporations;
  • synthetic protein companies;
  • urban nutrient recovery authorities.

Corporate Archipelago

Not a single alliance. A discontinuous web of concessions, ports, clinics, datacenters, model havens, security corridors, and work towns inside many countries.

This is how corporations become globally powerful without needing to replace the map.

Countries That Could Be Officially Corporate-Run

Use very sparingly. It is more plausible that cities, ports, corridors, and ministries are corporatized than whole large countries.

Best candidates for official or near-official corporate rule:

  • small island states trading jurisdiction for survival finance;
  • microstates becoming arbitration/data/model havens;
  • climate-bankrupt city-states;
  • port states under long-term concession;
  • special administrative zones;
  • mineral corridor authorities;
  • privately financed relocation capitals.

Better phrase than “bought out”:

  • under climate receivership;
  • under continuity administration;
  • under concessionary stabilization;
  • under emergency infrastructure compact;
  • under sovereign services agreement;
  • under corporate trusteeship, if intentionally sinister.

What Should Not Exist Anymore?

Avoid too many disappearances. But some categories may change.

Fully Sovereign Policy Autonomy

Many states still exist but cannot act freely because bond markets, insurers, climate treaties, water compacts, compute access, and platform identity systems constrain them.

Cheap Universal Insurance

Insurance becomes territorial power. Insurer withdrawal can be as politically important as invasion.

Clear Civilian / Corporate / Military Separation

Many places blur public police, private security, emergency managers, corporate infrastructure guards, and military logistics.

Universal Internet Assumption

Access to trusted reality becomes regional. Some countries have certified feeds, some have polluted feeds, some have Free-Net alternatives, and some have luxury provenance.

Unquestioned Coastal Permanence

Coasts still exist, but maintenance status matters more than shoreline shape.

Nation as the Only Identity Layer

Citizenship remains important, but people also belong to employer-clinics, model jurisdictions, migration permit classes, body-subscription tiers, Free-Net archives, faith movements, and Champion followings.

Design Recommendation

Keep the familiar map.

Then corrupt it with overlays.

Player-facing world map could show:

  1. Normal political map: countries and capitals.
  2. Maintenance map: maintained / sacrificed / contested.
  3. Corporate map: concessions, receiverships, bonded cloud zones, clinics, security corridors.
  4. Climate map: flood, heat, rainfall, salinity, fire, monsoon, Arctic route risk.
  5. Resource map: chips, compute, cooling, water, food, minerals, bodies.
  6. Reality map: certified feeds, polluted feeds, Free-Net, Digital-Net anomalies.
  7. Faction map: who actually holds power.

This directly addresses the concern: the world should contain France, Nigeria, India, Brazil, China, Mexico, Egypt, Bangladesh, etc. The strange labels describe the new layers of power inside and across them.

Open Questions

  • Which current countries must remain especially recognizable for player orientation?
  • Should the EU survive as the EU, become a harsher climate union, or partially fragment?
  • Should the United States remain federal but strained, or visibly lose control over some climate/corporate zones?
  • Should China be a White Sky co-architect, rival maintenance pole, or opponent of Western corporate sky control?
  • What is Russia’s 2052 state: continuity great power, patchwork petro-security state, or partial federation breakdown?
  • How central should India be to White Sky politics?
  • Should Nigeria be the main concrete West African anchor for White Sky opposition?
  • Should any country be fully corporate-administered, or should corporate rule mostly happen through cities/zones/corridors?
  • Which island or delta states become legally fascinating because their territory, population, and sovereignty no longer align?
  • How many formal border changes are desirable before the map feels too unrecognizable?
