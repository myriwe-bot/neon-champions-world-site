World Map and Political Economy Recommendations — 2026-05-20

Purpose

This page preserves the working recommendations from the 2026-05-20 brainstorming pass on what the Neon Champions world should look like after the white-sky, global warming, corporate expansion, resource stress, and climate-political upheaval.

This is not approved canon. It is a ponderable synthesis page: useful material should later be promoted into world-map-and-political-economy, detailed-world-map-seed, faction pages, location pages, mysteries, or design bridges.

Highest-Level Recommendation

The strongest map model is not “countries versus megacorps.” It is a layered world.

States still exist. Borders, passports, elections, militaries, myths, and national grudges still matter. But daily life is shaped by additional jurisdictions layered on top of those states:

  • climate-service territories;
  • insurer risk zones;
  • corporate concessions;
  • private-security districts;
  • migration permit zones;
  • water compacts;
  • compute/energy priority districts;
  • body-subscription eligibility regions;
  • platform identity jurisdictions;
  • Digital-Net / Free-Net shards.

A character can cross ten borders without leaving one city.

This keeps the world recognizable enough to be plausible, but unlike our own in the way people actually experience power.

What the Map Should Show Eventually

The visual game map does not need to be solved yet. The lore map can develop first.

Possible future map layers:

  • national borders;
  • climate-maintenance zones;
  • albedo impact / rainfall compensation zones;
  • corporate concession territories;
  • energy, compute, and cooling corridors;
  • migration routes and work-residency towns;
  • uninsurable or sacrificed regions;
  • port/cable/logistics chokepoints;
  • Digital-Net and Free-Net control layers;
  • faction influence overlays;
  • Champion incident sites.

The useful design question is not “where are the countries?” but “which layer matters to the player right now?”

Maintained, Sacrificed, and Contested Territories

The world should be divided less by formal sovereignty and more by maintenance status.

Maintained Territories

Places where infrastructure still works because someone pays, profits, governs, insures, or fights to keep it working.

Examples:

  • protected city cores;
  • ports and cable hubs;
  • elite arcology districts;
  • food-logistics corridors;
  • corporate agriculture zones;
  • fab and compute basins;
  • desalination corridors;
  • White Sky maintenance sites;
  • high-asset coastal metros;
  • insured climate-service districts.

These places save lives, but survival is contractual.

Common feel:

  • clean air and filtration;
  • cooling rights;
  • provenance-certified feeds;
  • biometric identity;
  • private security;
  • polished public interfaces;
  • opaque allocation dashboards;
  • body subscriptions tied to employment or residency.

Sacrificed Territories

Places the dominant system has chosen not to fully protect, even when they remain inhabited and culturally alive.

Examples:

  • salinity belts;
  • failed polders;
  • uninsurable suburbs;
  • polluted extraction districts;
  • informal settlements beyond cooling grids;
  • migration holding zones;
  • drought/flood rotation zones;
  • places too low-value to maintain but too populated to ignore.

These should not be written as passive wastelands. They are where resistance, black markets, local governance, Free-Net archives, improvised medicine, folk politics, and non-corporate cultures persist.

Contested Territories

Places whose future maintenance status is being fought over.

Examples:

  • deltas with half-built seawalls;
  • mineral basins;
  • dam and river corridors;
  • work-residency towns;
  • city receiverships;
  • Arctic shipping routes;
  • launch sites;
  • datacenter/cooling concessions;
  • White Sky maintenance corridors.

These are the best campaign spaces: unstable, strategic, morally ambiguous, and full of overlapping claims.

Who Benefited from the White Sky?

The strongest answer is not “the north” and not “evil corporations only.”

Recommended answer:

High-asset temperate and subtropical corridors benefited most because they could convert reduced heat risk into stability.

Likely sincere defenders:

  • North Atlantic finance and insurance states;
  • protected European coastal/metropolitan regions;
  • North American cities with climate debt exposure;
  • Pacific manufacturing, fab, port, and logistics corridors;
  • Gulf/desert engineering states selling cooling, desalination, and climate services;
  • corporate agriculture and food-logistics zones;
  • cities that would otherwise have become financially uninsurable.

Key nuance:

Many ordinary people in these places genuinely believe White Sky saved their parents, hospitals, homes, crops, and cities. They are not merely corporate dupes.

The injustice is subtler:

White Sky redistributes climate risk globally, and powerful regions are better at converting uncertainty into protection, liability shields, and profit.

This creates stronger political conflict than a simple villain/ victim split.

Who Was Harmed or Radicalized?

Recommended main opposition region:

West Africa + the Sahel + Gulf of Guinea.

Why this is strong:

  • West African monsoon sensitivity;
  • Sahel rainfall volatility;
  • farmer / pastoralist conflict pressure;
  • Lake Chad hydrology;
  • Lagos and Gulf of Guinea coastal flooding;
  • youth population and migration politics;
  • strong solar potential harmed by dimming / brightened sky;
  • resource corridors tied to batteries, compute, and global climate infrastructure;
  • credible climate-justice and atmospheric-sovereignty politics.

Do not make Africa uniformly anti-White-Sky.

Better internal split:

  • city health ministries may support White Sky because heat kills people;
  • farmers and pastoralists may oppose it because rainfall timing shifted;
  • coastal communities resent it because seas still rose;
  • solar utilities resent lost direct sunlight and reduced generation economics;
  • mining elites accept climate relief in exchange for concessions;
  • activists call it atmospheric occupation;
  • youth movements split between migration, reform, sabotage, and corporate employment.

Use Monsoon Timing, Not Generic Desertification

Avoid the stale version: “Africa became desert.”

Use a more specific and plausible wound:

  • farmers plant when the rains should arrive;
  • the rains delay;
  • seed fails;
  • late rains arrive as destructive floods;
  • insurers call it natural variability;
  • corporate climate models deny attribution;
  • local movements say the sky was moved without consent.

This is better because the conflict is about timing, uncertainty, compensation, and power — not just heat.

In-world slogan possibility:

You cooled the noon and stole the season.

Corporate Regions Worldwide

Corporate power should be common worldwide but unevenly distributed.

Large corporate presence should concentrate where survival infrastructure is expensive, strategic, or debt-financed:

  • lower-income resource extraction centers;
  • climate-bankrupt cities;
  • ports and cable chokepoints;
  • mineral rail corridors;
  • water/desalination systems;
  • datacenter/power basins;
  • biosecure clinic/agri zones;
  • migration work-residency towns;
  • White Sky maintenance sites;
  • disaster-hit rich-city districts under emergency concession.

Small corporate presence should be almost everywhere:

  • clinics;
  • cooling shelters;
  • company housing blocks;
  • bonded cloud rooms;
  • seed/fertilizer depots;
  • private-security service zones;
  • body-subscription counters;
  • platform identity offices;
  • feed provenance kiosks.

Backwaters should absolutely exist.

Types of backwater:

  • low-value regions not worth maintaining;
  • uninsurable interiors;
  • places too data-corrupt to govern actuarially;
  • local resistance zones;
  • remote cultures not connected to strategic corridors;
  • areas where informal systems work better than corporate services.

Backwaters are important because they prevent the setting from becoming “everything is corp-owned.” They are where outlaw clinics, old institutions, Free-Net weirdness, local Champions, and non-standard politics survive.

Corporate Region Archetypes

Tier 1: Most Central

Climate Migration Work-Towns

Best for:

  • housing and labor politics;
  • family separation;
  • permit economies;
  • body subscriptions;
  • climate debt;
  • private security;
  • outsourced border enforcement.

These are company towns for the climate era: not prisons on paper, but difficult to leave because work, medicine, identity, food, school, and legal status are bundled.

Debt-Receivership Cities

Best for showing corporate rule without invasion.

The city still has a mayor, council, flag, elections, and courts. But budgets, cooling systems, flood defenses, ports, emergency policing, land use, identity systems, insurance, and public feeds are controlled by a receiver or consortium.

This is more plausible and more cyberpunk than a corporation simply declaring itself a country.

Critical Mineral / Infrastructure Concessions

Best for lower-income extraction cyberpunk.

Global climate salvation requires batteries, grids, chips, desalination, drones, satellites, and White Sky maintenance. The people living near the mines, refineries, rail corridors, tailings basins, and water intakes carry the bodily and political costs.

Tier 2: Important Support Forms

Biosecure Health Zones

Useful for biotech, mutation, pandemic memory, fertility, body contracts, and elite medicine.

Port / Cable / Logistics Corridors

Useful for trade, smuggling, data sovereignty, private navies, sanctions, and war.

Bonded Cloud / Compute Concessions

Useful for VI politics, model sovereignty, energy allocation, data law, and sanctions evasion.

Tier 3: Use Sparingly

Resilience Arcologies

Visually strong, but generic if overused.

Recommendation: use arcologies as elite districts inside larger messy cities rather than as the default city form.

Compute Freeports / Bonded Cloud Zones

A compute freeport is like a customs freeport, but for computation, model weights, chips, data, cooling, energy, and jurisdiction.

Possible legal features:

  • chip import exemptions;
  • special water and energy rights;
  • tax sheltering;
  • model-weight storage under special law;
  • arbitration courts;
  • data-sovereignty carve-outs;
  • sanctions gray zones;
  • biometric worker housing;
  • private security;
  • climate-model hosting;
  • VI allocation quotas.

Better generic terms:

  • Bonded Cloud Zone;
  • Model Freeport;
  • Compute Concession;
  • Sovereign Cloud Concession;
  • Data Entrepôt;
  • Cloud Entrepôt;
  • Exempt Compute District;
  • Model Haven, as slang.

Example labels:

  • Mombasa Sovereign Cloud Port;
  • Kinshasa Data Entrepôt;
  • Tema Bonded Cloud Zone;
  • Zambezi Compute Concession.

Recommendation: use “compute freeport” as an explanatory category, but prefer more concrete in-world labels when naming actual places.

Blue Week as Campaign Catalyst

blue-week should likely be a real event, not only a feed hoax.

Strong version:

Eco-anarchist sabotage or atmospheric counter-intervention disrupts part of the White Sky maintenance lattice. For several days, some regions see real blue sky.

This should be beautiful, politically clarifying, and possibly murderous.

The eco-anarchists should not be dumb anti-science primitivists. Their critique is:

A livable planet has been converted into a corporate hostage system.

They know the White Sky prevents mass death. That is exactly why they believe proprietary control over it is intolerable.

Possible movement layers:

  • atmospheric commons lawyers;
  • Blue Restorationist mass politics;
  • Open Sky sabotage cells;
  • Unceiling networks leaking sky-model data;
  • rural and religious sky-return movements;
  • local climate-justice groups in rainfall-disrupted zones.

Opponents call them Terminationists because a large-scale failure could cause heat rebound and mass casualties. This label should be partly propaganda and partly fair warning.

Braided Campaign Crisis

Do not pick one isolated disaster yet. Use a braided crisis.

Recommended sequence:

  1. Blue Week happens.
  2. Sky Loyalists demand emergency powers to secure White Sky maintenance.
  3. West African rainfall claimants argue the sabotage revealed hidden tuning records.
  4. Corporate receivers move to protect ports, bonded cloud zones, and migration work-towns.
  5. Free-Net archivists leak contradictory climate-model fragments.
  6. Body-rights and labor unrest ignites in a work-town or receiver city.
  7. Champions become visible because institutions cannot resolve the crisis.

This sequence connects:

  • White Sky politics;
  • Africa / lower-income injustice;
  • corporate power;
  • Free-Net;
  • body subscriptions;
  • Champions;
  • climate science uncertainty;
  • factional conflict.

It is stronger than a single “world-ending event” because every faction can plausibly argue that it is preventing catastrophe.

Wars and Conflicts to Consider

Rainfall Attribution Conflict

A legal, insurgent, and propaganda conflict over whether White Sky tuning altered monsoon timing.

Possible forms:

  • climate tribunals;
  • sabotage of sensor networks;
  • data leaks;
  • compensation claims;
  • assassinations of model auditors;
  • rural militias targeting corporate weather stations;
  • states refusing atmospheric-service fees.

Port Continuity Wars

Ports remain valuable even when surrounding neighborhoods flood or collapse.

Possible forms:

  • private navies;
  • port-city receivership;
  • floating informal markets;
  • anti-cable sabotage;
  • migration clampdowns;
  • labor strikes by augmented dockworkers.

Mineral Corridor Wars

Not simply fights over mines, but over the rail, water, security, and arbitration systems around them.

Possible forms:

  • concession militias;
  • state reclamation campaigns;
  • worker mutation scandals;
  • tailings poisoning;
  • drone-protected rail spines;
  • Free-Net exposure of concession contracts.

Cooling Rights Unrest

In heat-stressed cities, cooling becomes a political right, labor condition, and riot trigger.

Possible forms:

  • work stoppages during wet-bulb alerts;
  • black markets in cooling credits;
  • clinic triage scandals;
  • body-heat certification fraud;
  • school and eldercare disasters;
  • Champions intervening as public protectors or strikebreakers.

Compute Famine

A power, chip, sanctions, or cooling crisis reduces access to VI services.

Possible forms:

  • collapse of automated bureaucracy;
  • private hoarding of model access;
  • emergency nationalization of datacenters;
  • black-market cognition;
  • Free-Net alternatives;
  • corporate claims that public safety requires compute rationing.

Natural Border Changes to Keep Grounded

Avoid arbitrary map scars. Use borders created by infrastructure, risk, and maintenance.

Good border types:

  • seawall lines;
  • pump districts;
  • salinity buffers;
  • wildfire insurance cutoffs;
  • wet-bulb work bans;
  • night-only road corridors;
  • river basin compacts;
  • dam release zones;
  • desalination intake territories;
  • port-security perimeters;
  • Arctic shipping control routes;
  • cloud-brightening or aerosol maintenance corridors;
  • migration permit belts;
  • corporate arbitration districts.

These feel plausible because they are recognizable extensions of real climate adaptation, insurance, logistics, and infrastructure politics.

Naming Discipline

Avoid names that sound like fantasy regions wearing cyberpunk sunglasses.

Prefer names that sound like:

  • treaties;
  • insurer maps;
  • concession contracts;
  • migration paperwork;
  • shipping manifests;
  • climate litigation;
  • activist slogans;
  • military logistics.

Useful forms:

  • Basin Security Area;
  • Rainfall Compensation District;
  • Albedo Impact Zone;
  • Coastal Protection Authority;
  • Hydrological Exception Zone;
  • Climate Stabilization Concession;
  • Bonded Cloud Zone;
  • Model Freeport;
  • Compute Concession;
  • Port Continuity District;
  • Receiver City;
  • Work-Residency Zone;
  • Carbon Custodial Territory;
  • Solar Curtailment Province.

Principle:

Cyberpunk is stronger when the horror arrives in paperwork.

Questions for User Review

These are the most important creative decisions to make later:

  1. Should West Africa / Sahel / Gulf of Guinea become the main anti-White-Sky moral center, or should opposition be more globally distributed?
  2. Which corporate archetype should be most player-facing: migration work-towns, receivership cities, mineral concessions, bonded cloud zones, or biosecure health districts?
  3. Should Blue Week be mostly eco-anarchist sabotage, corporate false flag, state experiment, maintenance failure, or deliberately unresolved?
  4. How much should ordinary citizens in maintained territories sincerely support White Sky?
  5. Are Champions born from crisis response, corporate programs, body-market inequality, Digital-Net contamination, or all of these?
  6. What should the first playable campaign region be: a receiver city, a rainfall tribunal zone, a port corridor, a mineral concession, or a work-town revolt?
  7. How visible should corporations be as rulers? Open flags and uniforms, or quiet control through contracts, insurers, platforms, and emergency authorities?
  8. Should the player ever be able to improve the White Sky system, or is it fundamentally a hostage infrastructure that can only be replaced or escaped?
