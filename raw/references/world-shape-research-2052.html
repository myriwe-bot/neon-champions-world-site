World Shape Research 2052

Purpose

This note grounds future Neon Champions world-map work in real climate, resource, and political-economy pressures. It is research scaffolding, not canon prose. The canon-facing synthesis is world-map-and-political-economy.

Core setting discipline: Neon Champions should not be “our world plus neon.” By ~2052 the map should feel visibly re-sorted by the white-sky, climate adaptation, corporate jurisdiction, compute/energy scarcity, resource corridors, migration, body economics, and polluted reality systems. The changes should be plausible and systemic, not random coolness.

Research Spine

Climate and geophysical pressures

  • Sea-level rise by mid-century is enough to reshape coasts even without extreme 2100 scenarios. NOAA/NASA’s 2022 U.S. technical report expects roughly 10-12 inches / 0.25-0.30 m of additional U.S. coastal sea-level rise by 2050, making damaging floods far more frequent. IPCC AR6 emphasizes compounding risks from subsidence, storms, erosion, heat, infrastructure lock-in, and uneven adaptation.

    • Sources: https://earth.gov/sealevel/us/resources/2022-sea-level-rise-technical-report/ ; https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/chapter/chapter-9/ ; https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg2/chapter/ccp2/
    • Fiction use: borders in coastal megacities become levee lines, pump districts, elevated rail spines, port-security zones, mortgage maps, and insurer red zones.

  • Low islands, deltas, and river mouths are stronger border-change engines than generic “the coast flooded.” They combine sea-level rise with subsidence, sediment starvation behind dams, salinization, groundwater extraction, storm surge, and food politics.

    • Sources: https://www.ipcc.ch/srocc/chapter/chapter-4-sea-level-rise-and-implications-for-low-lying-islands-coasts-and-communities/
    • Fiction use: Bangladesh/Ganges-Brahmaputra, Mekong, Nile, Mississippi, Chao Phraya/Bangkok, Jakarta/Java Sea, Pearl/Yangtze, Lagos/Niger Delta, Manila Bay, Alexandria, and Miami/Gulf Coast become laboratories for corporate polders, floating logistics, and juridical retreat.

  • Wet-bulb heat creates temporal borders. The land may remain politically present but become seasonally unworkable without cooling, shade, bodily modification, or curfews.

    • Sources: Persian Gulf humid heat research: https://www.nature.com/articles/nclimate2833 ; South Asia heat: https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.1603322 ; IPCC extremes: https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/chapter/chapter-11/
    • Fiction use: heat-curfew cities, night-only construction, cooling-credit checkpoints, shade-corridor tolls, evacuation subscription tiers, heat-pass labor permits, and poor districts where survival depends on rented endocrine/lung/skin packages from biotech-and-mutation.

  • Glaciers and snowpack retreat reshape rivers and mountain borders. The Hindu Kush Himalaya is a major credible hotspot for water timing, glacier-lake outburst floods, hydropower, and downstream agriculture.

    • Sources: https://hkh.icimod.org/hi-wise/ ; IPCC cryosphere: https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/chapter/chapter-9/
    • Fiction use: retreating ice exposes old survey lines, mines, crash sites, sacred routes, and new passes. Borders become drone-cadastral rather than just natural.

  • AMOC collapse should not be baseline by 2052. More grounded: AMOC weakening, North Atlantic weather weirding, fishery shifts, insurance panic, and political exploitation of tail-risk models.

    • Source: https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/chapter/chapter-9/
    • Fiction use: Europe and North Atlantic blocs argue over whether White Sky tuning is protecting them, harming them, or hiding a deeper oceanic transition.

  • Monsoon change is best used as timing/volatility rather than simple drying. Aerosols, greenhouse warming, land-use change, and natural variability can alter rainfall timing, intensity, and distribution.

    • Source: https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/chapter/chapter-8/
    • Fiction use: “monsoon theft” accusations against White Sky operators, dam-release crises, synchronized drought/flood years, and crop futures manipulated through synthetic-reality-and-polluted-feeds.

  • Arctic routes and resources are plausible mid-century flashpoints, but “ice-free Arctic” means low sea-ice extent, not a warm safe ocean.

    • Sources: https://www.climate.gov/news-features/understanding-climate/five-things-understand-about-ice-free-arctic ; https://arctic.noaa.gov/report-card/
    • Fiction use: Northern Sea Route, Northwest Passage, Greenland, Svalbard, Bering Strait, Arctic undersea cables, indigenous sovereignty, search-and-rescue monopolies, and climate-control bases become strategic.

  • Fire, drought, desertification, and smoke create moving borders based on evacuation zones, uninsurable property, water depots, fire perimeters, and grid access.

    • Sources: https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg2/ ; https://www.unccd.int/resources/publications
    • Fiction use: smoke-filter corridors become privatized lifelines; wildfire districts become insurer-administered security regions; burned towns are rebuilt as labor compounds or abandoned to Free-Net ghosts.

  • Climate migration is mostly internal/regional first, not only dramatic international invasion imagery. The World Bank Groundswell report estimates up to 216 million internal climate migrants by 2050 across six regions without strong action.

    • Source: https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2021/09/13/climate-change-could-force-216-million-people-to-migrate-within-their-own-countries-by-2050
    • Fiction use: residency, cooling access, water access, platform identity, and employment contracts become borders.

Corporate jurisdiction and extraterritoriality

The most plausible path to corporate territory is layered delegation, not cartoon sovereignty. The formula:

distressed public balance sheets + special jurisdiction + infrastructure concession + insurance/debt leverage + private security + emergency powers + arbitration protection = corporate region under nominal state sovereignty.

  • Special economic zones provide the legal chassis. UNCTAD’s 2019 World Investment Report counted nearly 5,400 SEZs across 147 economies, with more in development.

    • Sources: https://unctad.org/publication/world-investment-report-2019 ; World Bank/FIAS SEZ report: https://documents.worldbank.org/en/publication/documents-reports/documentdetail/343901468330977533/special-economic-zones-performance-lessons-learned-and-implications-for-zone-development
    • Fiction use: 2052 “resilience,” “compute,” “biosecurity,” “green industry,” and “port continuity” zones have separate civil rules, tax codes, labor contracts, data laws, immigration gates, and dispute systems.

  • Charter-city/model-city experiments and special courts show how alien legal systems can sit inside states. Honduras ZEDEs/Prospera, Dubai International Financial Centre Courts, and Astana International Financial Centre Court are useful real patterns.

    • Sources: https://www.difccourts.ae/ ; https://court.aifc.kz/
    • Fiction use: a corporate district may use local criminal law, foreign commercial law, platform terms of service, climate-insurance protocols, and investor-state arbitration at once.

  • Investor-state arbitration and stabilization clauses create soft sovereignty. The state may retain flags and police, but policy reversal becomes financially ruinous.

    • Sources: https://icsid.worldbank.org/ ; https://www.energychartertreaty.org/
    • Fiction use: citizens can vote to revoke a corporate charter and still lose because the damages award would collapse public services.

  • Infrastructure concessions become territorial administration when the operator controls roads, ports, water, pumps, grids, hospitals, broadband, clinics, evacuation, drones, and identity systems.

    • Fiction use: “lifeline operators” administer flooded or burned districts for 50-99 years.

  • Insurance retreat and adaptation finance become governance. Places can become uninhabitable in financial systems before they are physically lost.

    • Sources: https://firststreet.org/ ; https://www.fema.gov/grants/mitigation/building-resilient-infrastructure-communities ; insurer/reinsurer climate risk portals such as Munich Re and Swiss Re.
    • Fiction use: insurer-certified green zones, sensor mandates, managed-retreat covenants, hardening liens, and parametric disaster payouts decide whether residents can borrow, rent, work, or receive emergency services.

  • Private security is delegated coercion, not just private armies. The Montreux Document and industry codes acknowledge PMSCs as existing governance actors.

    • Source: https://www.icrc.org/en/publication/0996-montreux-document-private-military-and-security-companies
    • Fiction use: licensed public-safety partners operate drones, checkpoints, exclusion zones, and emergency deputization while formal police remain underfunded.

  • Debt, municipal receivership, and bailout logic make corporate-state hybrids plausible. Insolvent cities can accept receivers, asset sales, and service concessions after repeated climate shocks.

    • Sources: Puerto Rico PROMESA oversight: https://oversightboard.pr.gov/ ; U.S. TARP records: https://home.treasury.gov/data/troubled-assets-relief-program
    • Fiction use: a “Debt-Receivership City” is not conquered; it is refinanced into corporate administration.

Strategic resources and infrastructure

  • Energy grids and datacenters are core world-map logic. IEA Electricity 2024 reports data centres, AI, and crypto used about 460 TWh in 2022 and could exceed 1,000 TWh in 2026; grid buildout is a bottleneck for electrification and renewables.

    • Sources: https://www.iea.org/reports/electricity-2024 ; https://www.iea.org/reports/electricity-grids-and-secure-energy-transitions
    • Fiction use: compute blocs form around power-purchase treaties, hydropower basins, nuclear campuses, transformer supply, HVDC corridors, and cooling water. virtual-intelligence-and-compute-scarcity becomes map geography, not just background economics.

  • Cooling is strategic infrastructure. Space cooling, desalination, flood defense, smoke filtration, and White Sky maintenance decide who can live and work.

    • Sources: https://www.iea.org/reports/the-future-of-cooling ; https://www.unep.org/resources/global-cooling-watch-2023
    • Fiction use: states and corporations sell “habitable hours.” Sabotaging cooling grids during heat waves is equivalent to mass-casualty warfare.

  • Water remains one of the cleanest war/alliance drivers. World Bank estimates over 2 billion people lack safely managed drinking water; WRI says 25 countries face extremely high water stress and about 4 billion face high water stress at least one month a year.

    • Sources: https://www.worldbank.org/en/topic/water/overview ; https://www.wri.org/insights/highest-water-stressed-countries
    • Fiction use: upstream dam operators are military actors; desalinated-water exporters trade blue credits; aquifer pipeline states gain leverage.

  • Food and fertilizer are less flashy than chips but politically decisive. FAO tracks food insecurity and price shocks; fertilizer, water, ports, cold chains, and energy define food security.

    • Source: https://www.fao.org/publications/home/fao-flagship-publications/the-state-of-food-security-and-nutrition-in-the-world/en
    • Fiction use: calorie treaties, grain-port blockades, seed DRM insurgencies, synthetic-protein vats, and sewage nutrient recovery.

  • Rare earths, lithium, cobalt, nickel, copper, phosphate, potash, and helium are major strategic anchors.

    • USGS 2024 notes: rare earth mine production is heavily China-centered; lithium production/reserves anchor Australia/Chile/China; cobalt is heavily DRC-centered; nickel is Indonesia-centered; Morocco/Western Sahara dominates phosphate reserves; helium is concentrated around US/Qatar/Algeria/Russia-linked gas systems.
    • Sources: USGS 2024 Mineral Commodity Summaries: https://www.usgs.gov/centers/national-minerals-information-center/mineral-commodity-summaries
    • Fiction use: magnet famines immobilize drones/prosthetics; cobalt price spikes trigger limb repossessions; phosphate politics become food sovereignty; helium scarcity constrains quantum compute, medical imaging, superconducting grids, and fabs.

  • Semiconductors are not just Taiwan. The entire stack matters: advanced fabs, water, power, helium/neon, photoresists, EUV tools, packaging, EDA/IP, trusted technicians, and cybersecure logistics.

    • Source: SIA/BCG semiconductor supply-chain report: https://www.semiconductors.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/BCG-x-SIA-Strengthening-the-Global-Semiconductor-Value-Chain-April-2021_1.pdf
    • Fiction use: “chip neutrality” becomes a diplomatic status; body-control chips and outlaw VIs run on smuggled relic wafers.

  • Subsea cables and repair fleets are cyberpunk borders. Landing stations, chokepoints, cable maps, and repair ships decide latency, feed quality, financial clearing, and military coordination.

    • Sources: https://www.submarinecablemap.com/ ; https://www2.telegeography.com/submarine-cable-map
    • Fiction use: cable landing states become toll kingdoms; “accidental” anchor cuts create deniable wars; poor regions suffer thin-route reality pollution.

  • Space/launch infrastructure becomes climate-control infrastructure because orbital sensors, communications, launch corridors, and possible reflectors are part of White Sky governance.

    • Sources: https://www.faa.gov/data_research/commercial_space_data ; https://www.esa.int/Enabling_Support/Space_Transportation/Europe_s_Spaceport
    • Fiction use: launch workers are sky-maintenance citizens; orbital debris storms create diplomatic no-go lanes.

Plausible 2025-2052 Escalation Ladder

2025-2032: Stress normalizes exception

  • Insurance retreat accelerates in flood/fire/heat regions.
  • AI and datacenter growth turn grid access into industrial policy.
  • Green industry, chip, biotech, and logistics SEZs multiply.
  • Climate disasters create emergency procurement pipelines and data-sharing exceptions.
  • Migration remains mostly internal but strains city housing and identity systems.

2032-2040: Adaptation becomes debt

  • Municipalities and lower-income states cannot afford seawalls, cooling shelters, pump grids, desalination, and grid upgrades.
  • Adaptation banks, sovereign wealth funds, asset managers, insurers, cloud firms, and infrastructure operators package rescue deals.
  • Special jurisdictions expand from factories/ports to whole service districts.
  • Corporate housing, clinics, schools, private security, and ID become normal in work-residency compacts.

2040-2046: Resource shocks harden blocs

  • Battery minerals, copper, rare earth magnets, fertilizer, water, helium, and chips experience recurring shocks.
  • States designate strategic concession districts for minerals, water, geothermal, synthetic food, and compute.
  • Deltas, drylands, and heat basins produce recurring internal displacement.
  • Private security and insurers gain emergency authority in lifeline zones.

2046-2052: White Sky formalizes the new map

  • One corporate first mover crosses the planetary line and proves cooling dependency.
  • Governments denounce, copy, sue, regulate, contract, and eventually normalize the system because termination is too dangerous.
  • White Sky maintenance corridors, sensor basins, aerosol/launch ports, cloud-brightening fleets, climate models, and insurance ledgers become strategic targets.
  • Corporate Region Acts or equivalent laws license operators to administer service territories under nominal sovereignty.
  • The world map is now not just nations: it is states, climate blocs, corporate regions, exclusion zones, compute freeports, heat corridors, water compacts, insurgent commons, and Digital-Net shadows.

Border-Changing Occurrences That Fit Neon Champions

Use these as candidate historical incidents. They are designed to feel like White Sky: possible, unlikely, mystified, and institution-forming.

1. The First White Season

A corporate albedo deployment begins as “regional emergency stabilization” after synchronized crop failures and heat deaths. The first visible pale season cools enough to save harvests in some zones while disturbing rainfall elsewhere. The legal fight lasts years; the sky remains changed.

Political effect: treaties become retroactive; states split between Sky Loyalists and Blue Restorationists; the first company becomes hated savior.

2. The Delta Defaults

Several major deltas do not disappear; they financially default. Insurers, ports, food exporters, and adaptation banks divide them into protected cores, sacrificial salinity buffers, floating warehouses, and migrant exclusion belts.

Political effect: national borders remain, but delta residents live under polder authorities, port security, and climate-credit law.

3. The Monsoon Theft Accusation

A catastrophic monsoon failure/flood sequence is blamed on White Sky tuning. No tribunal can prove causation cleanly because climate models, sensor archives, and corporate datasets disagree.

Political effect: South/East Asian water alliances fragment; dam corridors militarize; pirate weather models gain credibility.

4. The Heat Curfew Decade

Several Gulf/South Asian/Red Sea cities adopt seasonal heat law: work, migration, policing, and market hours shift to night. Cooling-credit fraud becomes a major criminal economy.

Political effect: habitability becomes a subscription. Body-mod firms sell heat-tolerance packages as labor rights collapse.

5. The Arctic Insurance War

The Arctic opens just enough for profit and danger. A series of ship losses, cable cuts, and rescue failures leads insurers and private navies to define practical sovereignty through approved routes and rescue contracts.

Political effect: indigenous governments, Arctic states, shipping consortia, and climate-control authorities clash over who can certify risk.

6. The Cable Famine

A cluster of cable cuts near chokepoints causes feed degradation, banking delays, and VI outages across dependent regions. Public reality splinters by latency and provenance access.

Political effect: cable landing states and repair fleets become strategic powers; free-net archivists become local heroes and criminals.

7. The Phosphate Panic

A fertilizer shock after conflict/sanctions in phosphate or potash corridors triggers food-price riots. Urban sewage-mining and nutrient recovery become patriotic duty.

Political effect: food blocs replace some oil alliances; waste becomes strategic property; poor districts are surveilled as nutrient reservoirs.

8. The Limb Repo Crisis

A cobalt/nickel/magnet price spike makes low-tier subscription bodies unprofitable. Firms remotely disable, downgrade, or reclaim prosthetics and organ-support modules.

Political effect: body rights movements radicalize; Champions become symbols of bodily sovereignty or corporate enhancement.

9. The Receivership Coup

A climate-bankrupt city signs a resilience rescue package. The elected government remains, but taxes, utilities, police budgets, cooling shelters, land sales, and platform identity are controlled by a receiver consortium.

Political effect: official corporate rule arrives without tanks; residents argue whether rescue is occupation or survival.

10. The Blue Week

A temporary atmospheric clearing exposes real blue sky over multiple regions. Some call it system failure, miracle, sabotage, or proof the White Sky was never necessary.

Political effect: mass unrest, religious movements, corporate panic, market crashes, and faction recruitment. This could be a campaign-era event.

Plausible Global Political-Economic Geography

These are not hard canon names; they are map logics.

North Atlantic Climate Compact

Likely members/centers: parts of EU, UK/Ireland, Nordics, Canada, Northeast US, Greenland/Iceland nodes.

Power base: finance, insurance, offshore wind, nuclear/hydro, Arctic routes, climate courts, data regulation, certified feeds.

Internal contradiction: talks human rights and climate law while exporting actuarial exclusion, debt receivership, and sensor mandates.

White Sky stance: supports controlled maintenance but fears termination, AMOC weirding, and unauthorized tuning.

Fresh angle: not a clean liberal bloc; a lawyer-insurer-compute civilization that governs through standards and liability.

Pacific Compute-Manufacturing Mesh

Likely centers: Japan, Korea, Taiwan, coastal China fragments, Singapore/Malaysia/Vietnam, west coast North America, Australia.

Power base: semiconductors, rare-earth processing, robotics, drones, ports, subsea cables, battery supply chains, trusted hardware.

Internal contradiction: needs open trade and closed security at the same time.

White Sky stance: divided between coastal protection, monsoon risk, solar-yield losses, and chip-water security.

Fresh angle: sovereignty is supply-chain certification. A city can be politically small but globally untouchable because one cleanroom or cable hub anchors a whole VI ecosystem.

Equatorial Grievance Belt

Likely centers: West/Central/East Africa, parts of Amazonia, Southeast Asian wet tropics, northern South America, island states.

Power base: biodiversity, hydropower, carbon/biomaterial claims, cobalt/copper/lithium/bauxite/nickel, youth population, migration leverage.

Internal contradiction: exploited by old extractive deals but also home to new state-corporate elites profiting from concessions.

White Sky stance: accuses northern/corporate actors of monsoon theft, solar theft, and climate occupation.

Fresh angle: not merely victim regions; some become hard negotiators controlling minerals, bodies, biomaterials, and migration labor.

Desert Desalination and Solar States

Likely centers: Gulf, Red Sea, North Africa, parts of Australia, Atacama/Andean corridors, Southwestern North America.

Power base: desalination, solar, gas/hydrogen, helium/LNG, ports, sovereign wealth, controlled high-tech cities.

Internal contradiction: high-tech climate refuge sitting inside extreme ecological fragility and migrant labor dependence.

White Sky stance: resents dimming and altered solar yields; may demand compensation or local tuning rights.

Fresh angle: their wealth comes from manufacturing habitable hours, not only oil or solar exports.

Arctic-Subarctic Open Frontier

Likely centers: Canada, Russia fragments, Nordics, Greenland, Alaska, Iceland, Svalbard, Arctic indigenous polities.

Power base: shipping, minerals, fresh water, fishery shifts, launch/sensor stations, cold datacenters, undersea cables.

Internal contradiction: wants warming for access and cooling for global stability.

White Sky stance: strategically ambivalent; some factions oppose White Sky because it preserves ice and slows extraction.

Fresh angle: not a clean frontier but a litigious, dangerous, sensor-saturated insurance battlefield.

Continental Interior Granaries and Nutrient States

Likely centers: Canadian/US plains, Black Sea if stabilized, Brazil/Argentina, Central Asia, Northern China, parts of East Africa, Arctic fringe agriculture.

Power base: calories, seed IP, fertilizer, water timing, autonomous farm swarms, mycoprotein/fermentation factories.

Internal contradiction: food sovereignty rhetoric coexists with DRM seed monopolies and militarized export corridors.

White Sky stance: judges it by rainfall and crop yield, not ideology.

Fresh angle: fertilizer and data are as important as soil.

Corporate Archipelago

Not a single country; a discontinuous layer of zones: compute freeports, biosecure districts, port corridors, critical mineral protectorates, insurer-certified arcologies, migration work-towns, cloud campuses, launch territories, and White Sky maintenance bases.

Power base: contracts, infrastructure, courts, security, data, debt, and service dependency.

Internal contradiction: delivers functioning life support while hollowing citizenship.

White Sky stance: generally pro-maintenance, but corporations fight over tuning rights, liability, sensor access, and climate blame.

Fresh angle: the corporate map looks like beads on every national map, not a simple megacorp empire.

Free-Net / Exclusion Commons

Not a state; a messy layer of abandoned, uninsurable, insurgent, mesh-networked, floating, underground, and disaster-scarred territories.

Power base: illicit routing, archives, scavenging, smuggling, pirate weather models, body-hacking clinics, community protection, Champions outside law.

Internal contradiction: refuge and trap at once; truth commons mixed with malware, cults, scams, and faction propaganda.

White Sky stance: remembers what official feeds erase, but not always accurately.

Fresh angle: not pure rebellion. It is civilization’s cache, dump, immune system, and infection vector.

Corporate-Run Region Archetypes

Resilience Arcology District

Operator: insurer + construction giant + utility + city receiver.

What residents get: flood protection, cooling, clinics, secure work, verified feed, evacuation priority.

What residents lose: land autonomy, sensor privacy, unmanaged movement, political budget control.

Visual identity: vertical seawalls, shaded transit decks, white glare, green actuarial seals, blue-sky therapy rooms.

Compute Freeport

Operator: cloud/VI firm + power producer + sovereign wealth fund.

What residents get: jobs, bandwidth, education, clinic modeling, identity stability.

What residents lose: energy priority, data autonomy, freedom from score-based employment.

Visual identity: low-slung heat exchangers, guarded substations, silent drone patrols, temple-like cooling halls.

Biosecure Health Zone

Operator: biotech firm + hospital chain + public-health authority.

What residents get: pathogen protection, body leases, fertility services, organ support, heat adaptation.

What residents lose: genetic privacy, bodily autonomy, uncontrolled reproduction/treatment choices.

Visual identity: clean-room markets, living walls, filtered dawn light, cheerful consent kiosks.

Critical Minerals Protectorate

Operator: miner + security firm + export-credit agencies + host-state ministry.

What residents get: wages, roads, clinics, hazard pay, sometimes body upgrades.

What residents lose: environmental health, open labor organizing, resource sovereignty.

Visual identity: bright tailings under pale sky, armored haul roads, limb-repair clinics, foreign-language arbitration notices.

Port / Cable / Logistics Corridor

Operator: terminal company + customs tech + private police + telecom consortium.

What residents get: trade access, work, medicine, food imports, outbound migration brokers.

What residents lose: informal movement, labor privacy, political protest space.

Visual identity: elevated container rails, cable landing bunkers, customs drones, neon provenance stamps.

Climate Migration Work-Town

Operator: agri/compute/construction firm + immigration ministry + housing platform.

What residents get: legal presence, shelter, food, remittance rails, clinics.

What residents lose: right to remain after job loss, family autonomy, independent bargaining.

Visual identity: modular dorms, shade nets, biometric canteens, multilingual debt screens.

Visual World Rules

  • Daylight is wrong. Neon is visible in pale daytime because glare washes the world rather than cleanly illuminating it.
  • Borders are layered. A street can cross national, municipal, corporate, platform, insurer, security, and climate-service boundaries at once.
  • Rich places are over-maintained. Poor places are over-measured.
  • Blue is political: therapy, luxury, nostalgia, sabotage, mourning, or revelation.
  • Water, shade, cooling, bandwidth, and clean provenance are visible class markers.
  • Infrastructure is heraldry: pump towers, cable bunkers, cooling stacks, body clinics, drone buoys, polished substations, emergency sirens.
  • Abandoned zones are not empty. They contain mesh communities, illegal clinics, archive cults, autonomous maintenance leftovers, scavenger markets, and Digital-Net phenomena.
  • State flags still matter, but service logos matter more in daily life.

Recommended World Pillar Candidate

Working proposition:

The post-White-Sky world is not divided between nations and corporations. It is divided between maintained territories, sacrificed territories, and contested territories — and every faction claims a different right to decide which is which.

This fits Neon Champions because it generates:

  • strategy-map regions with different infrastructure logic;
  • factions as rival philosophies of survival/evolution;
  • Champions as jurisdictional crises and political symbols;
  • body subscriptions as survival infrastructure;
  • Digital-Net/Free-Net as both archive and battlefield;
  • wars over corridors, not just capitals;
  • corporations powerful without needing cartoon world conquest.

Open Research Questions

  • Which corporation made the first White Sky move: climate/insurance, agri-food, cloud/compute, aerospace, chemicals, logistics, or a consortium disguised as one firm?
  • Which regions benefited enough from White Sky to defend it sincerely?
  • Which regions were harmed enough to make Blue Restoration or Albedo Sovereignty a mass politics?
  • Are corporate regions legally common worldwide, or still scandalous exceptions?
  • Is there one recognized international climate court, or many competing climate tribunals?
  • What is the most player-visible map layer: nations, factions, corporate zones, climate corridors, or Digital-Net territories?
